What a time-duration calculator helps you find

Many everyday tasks are easier to plan when a clock interval is converted into hours and minutes. You may want to calculate how long a work shift lasted, how much time passed during a study session, the length of a meeting, or the time spent travelling. This calculator takes a start time and an end time, then reports the elapsed duration in hours and minutes.

It works with clock times rather than full date-and-time stamps. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator assumes the end time is on the following day. That makes it useful for an overnight shift or a journey that crosses midnight. It does not calculate breaks, time zones or a span longer than one day.

The formula

duration in minutes = end time in minutes − start time in minutes

To use the formula, first convert each clock time into minutes after midnight. Multiply the hour by 60 and add the minute value. Subtract the start total from the end total. If the answer is negative, add 1,440 minutes, because a full day contains 24 × 60 = 1,440 minutes.

Finally, divide the total minutes by 60. The whole-number part is the number of hours, and the remainder is the number of minutes.

hours = floor(total minutes ÷ 60)

remaining minutes = total minutes modulo 60

Worked examples

Example 1: A normal workday

Start: 08:45. End: 17:20.

Convert both times: 08:45 = 8 × 60 + 45 = 525 minutes. 17:20 = 17 × 60 + 20 = 1,040 minutes.

1,040 − 525 = 515 minutes

515 minutes is 8 hours and 35 minutes. The calculator displays 8h 35m.

Example 2: A shift that crosses midnight

Start: 23:15. End: 06:40.

Because 06:40 is earlier on the clock than 23:15, the calculator treats it as the next day. From 23:15 to midnight is 45 minutes. From midnight to 06:40 is 6 hours and 40 minutes.

0h 45m + 6h 40m = 7h 25m

Example 3: A short appointment

Start: 12:00. End: 12:45. The difference is 45 minutes, so the result is 0h 45m. A duration can be less than one hour.

Example 4: A session with a break

A study session runs from 09:10 to 12:00. The total span is 2 hours 50 minutes. If a 20-minute break was taken, the focused study time is 2h 50m − 20m = 2h 30m. The calculator reports the full span; subtract the break separately.

Example 5: Equal start and end times

If both fields are 08:00, the result is 0h 0m. The calculator treats identical clock readings as no elapsed time, not as a full 24-hour day. To represent a full day, use a date-based calculation or add 24 hours manually.

How to subtract hours and minutes by hand

You can subtract clock times directly, but borrowing one hour is sometimes necessary. Consider 14:10 to 17:35. Subtract the minutes: 35 − 10 = 25. Subtract the hours: 17 − 14 = 3. The result is 3 hours 25 minutes.

Now consider 08:50 to 12:15. You cannot subtract 50 minutes from 15 minutes without borrowing. Rewrite 12:15 as 11 hours and 75 minutes. Then 75 − 50 = 25 minutes, and 11 − 8 = 3 hours. The duration is 3h 25m.

Another reliable method is to convert everything to minutes first. For 08:50, calculate 8 × 60 + 50 = 530 minutes. For 12:15, calculate 12 × 60 + 15 = 735 minutes. The difference is 205 minutes, or 3 hours 25 minutes. This method avoids confusion when the minute value in the end time is smaller.

Converting a duration into decimal hours

Some timesheets, invoices and payroll systems ask for decimal hours rather than hours and minutes. The minutes must be divided by 60, not simply written after a decimal point. For example, 7 hours 30 minutes is 7.5 hours because 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5. But 7 hours 15 minutes is 7.25 hours, not 7.15.

decimal hours = whole hours + (minutes ÷ 60)

A shift lasts 7 hours 50 minutes.

7 + (50 ÷ 60) = 7.8333… hours

Rounded to two decimal places, that is 7.83 hours. If the hourly rate is 2,000 currency units, the estimated gross amount for that duration is 7.8333… × 2,000 = 15,666.67. Actual payroll may use its own rounding and break rules.

Subtracting unpaid breaks

The calculator measures the elapsed span between the two times. It does not know whether the person stopped for lunch, took a rest break or left the workplace. If a break is unpaid, subtract it from the elapsed duration before calculating paid hours. If the break is paid, the employer's policy may treat it differently.

A shift runs from 08:30 to 17:00, a total of 8 hours 30 minutes. The worker takes a 30-minute unpaid lunch break. Paid time is 8h 30m − 0h 30m = 8h. If there are two separate unpaid breaks, add their lengths before subtracting them.

For several breaks, convert each to minutes, add the break minutes together and subtract the total from the elapsed minutes. This reduces mistakes when one break is 15 minutes and another is 35 minutes.

Using duration calculations for study and project planning

A study session is not always the same as productive study time. If a student studies from 16:00 to 18:30 and spends 20 minutes away from the desk, the total period is 2h 30m but focused time is 2h 10m. Recording both can help identify whether a schedule is realistic.

For project work, calculate the duration of each session and add the results to estimate total effort. Do not confuse elapsed time with calendar days: a two-hour task completed on Monday and another two-hour task on Wednesday represent four hours of effort across a three-day date interval.

If you need to divide available study minutes across several subjects, the Study Time Calculator can help. For a date interval that spans multiple days, use the Date Calculator.

Limits and situations that need extra care

  • One day at most. The calculator adds one day when the end clock time is earlier than the start time. It cannot identify a two-day or multi-day interval from clock times alone.
  • Same time means zero. Equal values return zero, not 24 hours. A full-day period needs dates or a separate 24-hour adjustment.
  • No date or time-zone information. The tool does not know which date the times belong to, or whether the locations use different time zones.
  • No daylight-saving adjustment. In places that change clocks, an overnight interval on the changeover date may be one hour shorter or longer in real elapsed time.
  • No automatic break deduction. The result is the full time between the two entries.
  • Minutes only. The inputs do not include seconds, so the result is not precise to the second.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up am and pm. Check whether the time is morning or evening. Entering 7:00 instead of 19:00 can change the result substantially.
  • Writing minutes as decimal fractions. 30 minutes is half an hour, not 0.30 hours. Divide minutes by 60 when converting to decimal time.
  • Forgetting the overnight rule. An earlier end time is treated as the following day, which is useful for overnight shifts but may be wrong if the times belong to different dates.
  • Counting breaks as paid time. The tool does not know which breaks are paid.
  • Expecting equal times to mean a full day. Equal entries produce zero.
  • Using a clock interval for a multi-day trip. Add dates or use the Date Calculator for longer spans.

What this calculator does and does not do

Enter a start time and end time. The calculator converts them into minutes after midnight, subtracts the start from the end, and adds 24 hours if the result is negative. It then displays the duration as whole hours and remaining minutes. It does not calculate pay automatically, deduct breaks, compare time zones or measure multiple days.

For an hourly-pay estimate, convert the result to decimal hours and multiply by the hourly rate, while checking the employer's payroll rules. The Salary Calculator converts yearly, monthly and weekly salary amounts, but it does not calculate an hourly rate directly.

Practice questions

  1. How long is the interval from 09:25 to 16:10?
  2. How long is an overnight shift from 22:40 to 05:15?
  3. Convert 6 hours 45 minutes into decimal hours.
  4. A workday lasts 9 hours 10 minutes and includes a 40-minute unpaid break. How much paid time remains?

Answers: 1. 6h 45m. 2. 6h 35m. 3. 6 + 45 ÷ 60 = 6.75 hours. 4. 8h 30m.

Time duration and attendance records

For a personal time log, it can be useful to record the start time, end time and any unpaid break separately. For example, a worker may record 07:55 as the start, 16:25 as the end, and 35 minutes as an unpaid break. The elapsed span is 8 hours 30 minutes. Subtracting the break gives 7 hours 55 minutes of paid time. Keep the original entries as well as the adjusted total so that the calculation can be checked later.

Workplaces may have rules about rounding clock-in times, grace periods, overtime and paid breaks. This calculator does not apply those policies. Use the official timesheet or employer's payroll system when the result affects wages, and follow the applicable employment agreement.

Adding several time intervals

If an activity is split into multiple sessions, calculate each interval and add the durations. Convert each result to minutes before adding, then convert the final total back to hours and minutes. This avoids treating 60 minutes as if it were a decimal hundred.

A person works for 2h 40m in the morning and 3h 35m in the afternoon. Add the minutes: 40 + 35 = 75 minutes, which is 1 hour 15 minutes. Add the hours: 2 + 3 + 1 carried hour = 6 hours. Total time is 6h 15m.

If you are adding many sessions, keep a simple table with one row per session. Check that no interval has been entered twice and that overnight sessions are assigned to the correct day. A clock-time calculator alone cannot detect overlapping entries.

Check the result before using it

A useful sense-check is to estimate the interval in two parts. Count from the start time to the next whole hour, then count the remaining hours and minutes to the end. For an overnight shift, split it at midnight. If the calculator's result is very different from that mental estimate, recheck the selected times, the am/pm setting and whether the end belongs to the following day.

For repeated shifts, use the same time format each time and record whether breaks are included. Consistent records make weekly totals easier to add and compare.

When a duration affects payment, compare the calculated interval with the approved attendance record before submitting it. A small entry error can be repeated across several shifts and affect the total.

For travel planning, remember that a clock interval does not include waiting time unless the start and end entries cover it. Add separate legs when needed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate time across midnight?

Enter the start and end clock times. If the end time is earlier, the calculator assumes it belongs to the following day and adds the overnight portion.

Can I enter seconds?

No. The calculator works with hours and minutes.

Does it subtract lunch or rest breaks?

No. It reports the full elapsed span. Subtract unpaid breaks separately if you need paid or focused time.

Why does the same start and end time show zero?

The calculator interprets identical clock readings as no elapsed time. It cannot tell whether you intended a full 24-hour period.

Can I calculate a shift that lasts more than 24 hours?

Not reliably with clock times alone. Use the dates as well, or split the period into separate intervals.

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?

Divide the minutes by 60 and add the result to the whole hours. For example, 20 minutes is 20 ÷ 60 = 0.3333 hours.
