Why compare salary by different pay periods?

Income is often discussed in different time units. A job advert may state an annual salary, a contractor may quote a weekly amount, and a household budget may be organised by month. Comparing these figures directly can be confusing because they describe different periods. This calculator converts one salary amount into yearly, monthly, weekly and daily equivalents so that the figures can be viewed side by side.

Enter the amount and select whether it is paid yearly, monthly or weekly. The calculator converts the entry to an annual amount first, then divides that amount into the other periods. It is a unit-conversion tool for pay, not a tax calculator, payslip generator or full employment-benefits comparison.

The assumptions and formulas

The calculator uses a simplified year containing 12 months, 52 weeks and 260 working days. The daily figure assumes five working days in each of 52 weeks.

yearly pay = entered yearly amount

yearly pay = monthly amount × 12

yearly pay = weekly amount × 52

Once the annual amount is known, the other results are calculated as follows:

monthly = yearly ÷ 12

weekly = yearly ÷ 52

daily = yearly ÷ 260

The same annual total is used for every conversion. This keeps the results consistent with the calculator's assumptions. Actual employment arrangements may use 13 monthly payments, 26 fortnightly payments, 4-weekly pay periods, irregular shifts or a different number of paid working days.

Worked examples

Example 1: Starting with annual pay

A job offers 48,000 per year.

  • Monthly: 48,000 ÷ 12 = 4,000.
  • Weekly: 48,000 ÷ 52 = 923.08.
  • Daily: 48,000 ÷ 260 = 184.62.

The yearly amount remains 48,000. The other figures are equivalent averages based on the calculator's 12-month, 52-week and 260-day assumptions.

Example 2: Starting with monthly pay

A worker earns 2,500 each month.

Yearly = 2,500 × 12 = 30,000

Weekly = 30,000 ÷ 52 = 576.92

Daily = 30,000 ÷ 260 = 115.38

Example 3: Starting with weekly pay

A worker is paid 600 each week.

Yearly = 600 × 52 = 31,200

Monthly = 31,200 ÷ 12 = 2,600

Daily = 31,200 ÷ 260 = 120

Example 4: Comparing two offers

Offer A pays 36,000 per year. Offer B pays 3,100 per month. Convert Offer B to a yearly equivalent: 3,100 × 12 = 37,200. On the calculator's assumptions, Offer B is 1,200 higher per year. Before deciding, compare working hours, leave, benefits, deductions, job security and any unpaid periods.

Example 5: Estimating an hourly equivalent

The calculator does not display hourly pay. If a weekly wage is 600 and the worker normally works 40 paid hours, divide 600 by 40. The hourly equivalent is 15. If the worker's hours vary, use the actual paid hours for the period rather than assuming every week is identical.

Why 52 weeks and 260 working days?

The calculator uses 52 weeks as a convenient annual conversion. Multiplying a weekly wage by 52 gives a standard annual equivalent. The daily amount is then based on five working days per week: 52 × 5 = 260 working days.

A calendar year has 365 days in a common year and 366 in a leap year, so 52 weeks is a simplified payroll convention rather than the exact number of calendar days in every year. It also does not account for public holidays, annual leave, sick leave, unpaid time off or a work pattern that includes weekends. The daily result is therefore an average conversion, not a promise that every workday is paid at that exact rate.

Gross salary and take-home pay

Gross salary is pay before deductions. Take-home pay, also called net pay, is the amount remaining after applicable taxes, pension contributions and other deductions. This calculator converts the amount you enter without deciding whether it is gross or net. If you enter a gross annual salary, all four results are gross equivalents. If you enter a net monthly amount, the converted figures are net equivalents.

Do not treat the monthly result as the amount that will necessarily arrive in your bank account. Payroll deductions, benefits, allowances and employer-specific arrangements can change the actual payment. To estimate a tax amount separately, use the Tax Calculator, while remembering that tax rules depend on the jurisdiction and personal circumstances.

Monthly, four-weekly, fortnightly and 13th-month pay

Not every employer pays on the same schedule. A monthly salary is usually paid 12 times in a year. Four-weekly pay is paid every 28 days, which creates 13 payment periods in a 52-week year. Fortnightly pay is paid every two weeks, giving 26 periods in 52 weeks. A 13th-month arrangement may provide an additional payment under the employer's policy or local practice.

These schedules should not be confused. If an amount is paid every four weeks, multiplying it by 12 will understate the annual equivalent because there are 13 four-week periods in 52 weeks. This calculator's choices are yearly, monthly and weekly only. Convert a four-weekly amount by multiplying by 13 before using it as an annual amount, or divide an annual amount by 13 to estimate a four-weekly payment.

If a four-weekly payment is 2,000, the simplified annual equivalent is 2,000 × 13 = 26,000. A monthly average of that annual amount is 26,000 ÷ 12 = 2,166.67. This is an average monthly equivalent, not the amount paid every four weeks.

If an employer pays an annual salary in 13 equal instalments, the instalment amount is annual salary divided by 13. It is not the same as the standard monthly equivalent, which divides by 12.

Comparing job offers fairly

Converting salary to a common period is only the first step in comparing two jobs. A higher headline salary may come with longer hours, a longer commute, fewer benefits or a less predictable schedule. A lower salary may include paid leave, health cover, pension contributions, bonuses or other benefits. The value of those items depends on the worker's situation and the terms of the offer.

  • Compare the same pay basis. Check whether each figure is gross or net and whether it is annual, monthly or weekly.
  • Check the number of paid periods. Confirm whether pay is monthly, fortnightly, four-weekly or based on shifts.
  • Compare working hours. A salary for 50 hours a week is not directly equivalent to one for 40 hours a week.
  • Check unpaid closures or breaks. Some roles have seasonal gaps or unpaid periods that affect actual annual income.
  • Include benefits and allowances carefully. Separate guaranteed pay from discretionary bonuses, reimbursements and conditional incentives.
  • Read the contract. Confirm how leave, overtime, probation, deductions and pay reviews are handled.

Using the currency selector

The currency selector in the page header changes the symbol used to display monetary figures. It does not convert the amount from one currency into another. If you enter 1,000 while the display is set to a different currency symbol, the calculator still performs the same arithmetic on the number 1,000; it does not look up an exchange rate.

For an actual currency conversion, use the Currency Converter and provide the exchange rate you want to apply. Exchange rates change over time, and a bank or payment provider may use a different rate or add fees.

Common salary-conversion mistakes

  • Choosing the wrong pay frequency. Entering a monthly amount while Yearly is selected makes every result inconsistent with the intended salary.
  • Confusing four-weekly and monthly pay. Four-weekly pay occurs 13 times in 52 weeks, while monthly pay occurs 12 times in a year.
  • Assuming the daily result is a contractor rate. The 260-day figure is a simple average and does not account for unpaid leave, expenses, downtime or business costs.
  • Mixing gross and net amounts. Compare gross with gross or net with net. Do not compare a pre-tax salary with a take-home amount.
  • Assuming every month has four weeks. A month is not exactly four weeks. The calculator uses annual pay divided by 12.
  • Thinking the currency selector performs exchange conversion. It changes the display symbol only.
  • Ignoring hours and benefits. Salary alone does not describe the full value or demands of a job.

What this calculator does and does not do

Enter a non-negative salary amount and select Yearly, Monthly or Weekly. The calculator converts the entry into an annual figure and shows annual, monthly, weekly and daily equivalents. The formulas use 12 months, 52 weeks and 260 working days.

It does not calculate tax, pension, insurance, bonuses, overtime, hourly pay or a legally required wage. It does not know whether the amount is gross or net, and it does not convert currencies. The output is a mathematical comparison based on the amount and pay frequency you select.

From daily pay to hourly pay

The calculator's daily figure is based on 260 working days, not on the number of hours in a shift. To estimate an hourly equivalent, divide the pay for the relevant period by the number of paid hours in that same period. The result changes when the work schedule changes, even if the annual salary stays the same.

Suppose an annual salary is 52,000 and the employee works 40 paid hours each week for 52 weeks. Weekly pay is 52,000 ÷ 52 = 1,000. The hourly equivalent is 1,000 ÷ 40 = 25. If the role requires 50 paid hours each week for the same annual amount, the simple hourly equivalent becomes 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20.

This is a comparison calculation, not a legal determination of an hourly wage. Overtime rules, salaried exemptions, unpaid breaks and statutory minimum rates vary by jurisdiction and employment arrangement. Use the applicable rules and contract when assessing actual compensation.

Irregular income and variable schedules

Some workers do not receive the same amount every week. Freelancers, commission-based employees, casual workers and people with changing shifts may have fluctuating income. Converting one week's pay into an annual figure by multiplying by 52 assumes that the same amount is earned every week for a full year. That assumption may be unrealistic when work is seasonal or contracts are short.

For variable income, collect several months of actual payments and calculate the total over the period. Divide by the number of months to find an average monthly amount, or by the number of weeks represented to find an average weekly amount. Keep unusually large bonuses and one-off reimbursements separate if they are not regular earnings. A simple salary conversion is most useful when the underlying pay is stable and the selected pay frequency accurately describes it.

When budgeting, it can be safer to base essential expenses on a conservative estimate rather than the best month. Keep provision for tax, business expenses, unpaid leave and gaps between contracts where those apply.

Frequently asked questions

How many working days are in the calculator's year?

It assumes 260 working days, calculated as 52 weeks multiplied by five working days. It does not subtract public holidays or leave.

Is monthly pay always yearly salary divided by 12?

This calculator uses that convention. An employer may use a different payment schedule, such as four-weekly or 13 instalments.

Does the calculator show hourly pay?

No. Divide the pay for a period by the actual paid hours in that period to estimate an hourly equivalent.

Does it calculate take-home pay?

No. It converts the amount entered and does not apply tax, pension or other deductions.

Does changing the currency symbol convert the salary?

No. The selector changes the displayed symbol only. Use a currency converter and a suitable exchange rate for a real conversion.

Can I use it to compare two job offers?

Yes, as a first step. Convert both offers to the same period and pay basis, then compare hours, benefits, leave, deductions and contract terms.
