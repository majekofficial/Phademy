Calculating a tip and splitting the bill

At the end of a meal there are usually three questions: how much is the tip, what is the total, and what does each person owe. Enter the bill, the tip percentage and the number of people, and the calculator answers all three. If you leave the number of people empty it assumes one person.

The formula

tip = bill × tip % ÷ 100

total = bill + tip

each person pays = total ÷ number of people

The tip is based on the bill amount you enter, so enter the figure before any tip or service charge has been added.

Worked examples

A quick tip

A bill of 60 with a 15% tip.

60 × 0.15 = 9 tip, 60 + 9 = 69 total

Splitting between three

A bill of 84.50 with an 18% tip shared by 3 people.

  • tip: 84.50 × 0.18 = 15.21
  • total: 84.50 + 15.21 = 99.71
  • each person: 99.71 ÷ 3 = 33.24

Three payments of 33.24 add up to 99.72, one cent more than the total, because shares are rounded to the nearest cent.

Estimating without a calculator

Ten percent is the bill divided by 10, and 5% is half of that. For a bill of 60, 10% is 6 and 5% is 3, so 15% is 9, the same as above.

Things worth knowing

  • Check for a service charge. Some restaurants add one automatically. If it is already on the bill, you may not need to tip as well.
  • Customs differ. Normal tip rates vary by country and even by city, from nothing to 20% or more. Use the percentage that suits where you are.
  • Tip on the pre-tax amount or the total. There is no universal rule. Entering the bill before tax gives a slightly smaller tip, which many people prefer.
  • Round for convenience. After calculating, you can round the total up to a neat figure and share the difference among the group.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a bill that already contains a tip, which results in tipping on a tip.
  • Dividing the bill by the group size and tipping separately, which makes uneven amounts when people ordered different things.
  • Typing 0.15 for a 15% tip. The field expects 15.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical tip?

It depends on the country and the type of service. Rates between 10% and 20% are common in places where tipping is customary, and a tip may not be expected elsewhere.

Does the calculator include tax in the bill?

Only if you include it in the bill amount. Enter the figure on which you want the tip based.

Can I split the bill unevenly?

Not directly. Calculate one total, then agree shares by hand, or run the calculator separately for each person's items.

Why do individual shares not add up exactly?

Each share is rounded to the nearest cent, so the sum can differ from the total by a cent or two.

What the tip calculator works out

A tip is an extra amount paid to recognise service. The calculator starts with a bill amount and a tip percentage, then calculates the tip, the combined total and the amount per person when the bill is shared equally. It is a simple percentage calculation. It does not decide what percentage is appropriate, check local tipping customs or determine whether a service charge has already been included. Those are decisions you make before entering the values.

The basic formula is tip = bill amount × tip percentage ÷ 100. The total is bill amount + tip. If the bill is divided equally among a group, each person's share is total ÷ number of people. Enter the percentage as a whole number: for a 15% tip, enter 15 rather than 0.15. The number of people field is used to divide the bill total, including the tip, equally.

Further bill-splitting examples

Calculating a 10 per cent tip

A bill is 80 and you choose to leave 10%. The tip is 80 × 10 ÷ 100 = 8. The total payment is 80 + 8 = 88. Ten per cent is easy to estimate mentally because it is one tenth of the bill. If the bill were 86.50, ten per cent would be 8.65 and the combined total would be 95.15.

Calculating an 18 per cent tip

Suppose a restaurant bill is 125. An 18% tip is 125 × 18 ÷ 100 = 22.50. Add the tip to the bill: 125 + 22.50 = 147.50. The tip is not 18 currency units; it is 18 out of every 100 units of the bill. The calculator performs this percentage step and shows the total.

Sharing a bill among four people

A group of four has a bill of 96 and decides on a 12.5% tip. The tip is 96 × 0.125 = 12. The combined total is 108. Divide by four people to get 27 each. This assumes everyone is contributing equally. If one person ordered substantially more than the others, an equal split may not feel fair. The calculator does not know what each person ordered, so it cannot produce an itemised or weighted split.

Sharing a bill when rounding creates a small difference

A bill of 84.50 with an 18% tip produces a tip of 15.21 and a total of 99.71. Dividing by three gives 33.2366… per person, which displays as 33.24 when rounded to two decimal places. Three payments of 33.24 add up to 99.72, one cent more than the exact total. One person can pay 33.23 while the other two pay 33.24 to make the contributions add up exactly. This is a normal consequence of rounding currency to the smallest unit.

Choosing the amount on which to tip

Some people calculate a tip on the pre-tax amount, while others use the full amount shown on the bill. There is no universal rule that applies in every country, business or situation. If you want the tip based on the pre-tax amount, enter that amount. If you want to tip on the final bill including tax, enter the final bill. The calculator uses exactly the amount you enter and does not automatically remove tax or service charges.

Before adding a tip, inspect the receipt for a service charge, gratuity or similar line. A service charge may already be distributed to staff, or it may be a fee retained by the business, depending on local rules and the establishment's policy. Do not assume that every extra charge is the same as a voluntary tip. If you are unsure, ask the business how the charge is handled. The calculator cannot interpret a receipt or determine who receives the money.

Understanding percentages and quick estimates

To calculate 15% mentally, find 10% and add half of that amount. For a bill of 60, ten per cent is 6 and five per cent is 3, so 15% is 9. To calculate 20%, double the 10% amount. To calculate 12%, find 10% and 2%, then add them. Two per cent is one fifth of 10%. These shortcuts are useful for checking a calculator result and estimating a tip when you do not have a device handy.

Another way to calculate the total directly is to multiply the bill by 1 plus the tip rate as a decimal. For a 15% tip, multiply by 1.15. A bill of 60 becomes 60 × 1.15 = 69. This method is mathematically equivalent to calculating the tip separately and adding it, but the calculator displays the tip and total as separate figures so you can see the breakdown.

Splitting costs fairly

An equal split is simple when everyone agrees to share the same amount. It works well for a group meal where people ordered similar items, a taxi fare shared by passengers travelling the same route or a shared service fee. It can be less suitable when one person ordered much more, someone did not use the service or the group agreed to pay different proportions. In those cases, calculate each person's share separately or agree on a fair allocation before collecting payment.

For a split across several payment methods, the total can still be calculated here, but you may need to distribute the rounded amounts manually. For example, if the total is 101 and three people are paying in whole currency units, shares of 34, 34 and 33 add up to 101. Avoid collecting the rounded-up share from every person if that would exceed the bill. Keep a record of the exact total and the contributions.

Practical situations beyond restaurants

Tipping practices differ widely. In some places, a tip is customary in restaurants and certain service industries. In others, service charges are included or tipping is uncommon. Delivery, hotel, transport, salon and other service situations may each have different expectations. Consider the service, local custom, business policy and your own budget. A calculator can show the arithmetic for any percentage, but it cannot tell you what you are expected or required to pay.

If you are calculating a tip for a service paid in cash, you may want to round the final amount to a convenient note or coin value. Work out the exact tip first, then decide whether to round up. Do not change the percentage field to force a convenient total without understanding how much extra that creates. If you want to compare the effect of different percentages, calculate each one separately and compare the tip and total.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Entering 0.15 when the field expects 15 for a 15% tip.
  • Adding a second tip without checking whether a service charge or gratuity is already on the bill.
  • Entering a bill amount that already includes a tip, then calculating a tip on top of it.
  • Forgetting to include the number of people when you want an equal split.
  • Assuming an equal split is fair when people ordered very different amounts.
  • Expecting rounded shares to add to the exact total without a small adjustment.
  • Assuming tipping customs are identical in every country or service setting.

What the calculator does not include

The calculator applies one percentage to one bill amount and divides the resulting total equally by the number of people. It does not calculate tax, remove a service charge, split individual menu items, account for coupons or apply different tip rates to different people. If a discount changes the bill, calculate the discounted bill first using the Discount Calculator, then enter the amount on which you want to tip. For a general percentage calculation, the Percentage Calculator can help with other percentage questions.

More tipping questions

How much should I tip?

There is no single worldwide answer. Common expectations vary by country, service and business. Check local custom and any service charge shown on the bill, then choose an amount that is appropriate for the situation and your budget.

Does the calculator include tax?

It includes tax only if tax is part of the bill amount you enter. It does not calculate or remove tax automatically.

Can I split the bill unevenly?

The built-in split is equal. For an uneven split, calculate the total tip and total bill first, then allocate the amounts according to what each person ordered or the arrangement your group agreed on.

Why does each person's share not add up exactly?

Each share may be rounded to two decimal places. The rounded shares can differ from the exact total by a small amount, so adjust one share by a cent if necessary.

Should I tip before or after tax?

Practices differ. Enter the pre-tax amount if that is the basis you choose, or the final bill if you prefer to calculate on the amount charged. The calculator does not make that choice for you.

Keeping a clear record for a group

When one person pays the full bill, write down the final amount and the agreed share before collecting transfers. If people pay separately, make sure the restaurant has not already split the payment in a way that changes the amount due. A quick check of the receipt, tip and total helps everyone understand what they are contributing and avoids asking the group to pay twice.
