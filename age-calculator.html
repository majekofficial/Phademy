Calculating your exact age in years, months and days

Forms, school applications, insurance quotes and legal thresholds often ask for an exact age rather than a birth year. Choose your date of birth and this calculator shows how long you have been alive, measured to today's date, in whole years, months and days.

How the age is counted

The calculator starts with the difference between today and your birthday, then adjusts for the unequal lengths of months. If today's day of the month is smaller than your birth day, it borrows a month and adds that month's days; if the month is smaller, it borrows a year. This is why it is more accurate than simply subtracting birth year from current year.

  • Birthdays count only when the date is reached, so someone born on 15 March has not yet turned 25 on 14 March.
  • Leap years are handled by the calendar itself.
  • A birth date in the future shows a dash, because no age can be calculated.

Additional age calculation examples

On a birthday

Born on 15 March 2000; today is 15 March 2025.

25 years, 0 months, 0 days

A few weeks later

Born on 15 March 2000; today is 10 April 2025.

From 15 March to 15 April would be one month, so you stop 5 days short of that: the result is 25 years, 0 months and 26 days (16 days left in March plus 10 in April).

A leap-day birthday

Born on 29 February 2024; today is 1 March 2025. The calculator shows 1 year, 0 months and 0 days. In years without a 29 February, the birthday is treated as falling on 1 March for this purpose. Laws and institutions may choose 28 February instead, so check the rule that applies to you.

Using your result

  • Eligibility checks. School entry, driving, voting and pension rules use a specific cut-off date, not just your age today. Check the date in the rules.
  • Planning. To find the days until your next birthday, set that date in the Countdown Calculator.
  • Anniversaries. The same method gives how long a relationship, job or company has existed.

Common mistakes

  • Entering day and month the wrong way round. Check the date picker shows the month you meant.
  • Assuming the result reflects another date. The calculator always measures to today's date on your device, so it will change tomorrow.
  • Using age in months for a baby without checking the months figure. The result gives years, months and days together.

How an exact age calculation works

An age calculator compares a date of birth with the current date and expresses the difference as completed years, remaining months and remaining days. This is different from dividing the total number of days by 365. A calendar year can contain 365 or 366 days, and months have different lengths. A calendar-based calculation therefore needs to borrow days from the previous month when the current day number is smaller than the birth day number.

Why the answer is years, months and days

Suppose a person was born on 12 April 2000 and the comparison date is 20 June 2026. The completed-year count is 26. The month difference from April to June is two, and the day difference from the 12th to the 20th is eight. The result is 26 years, 2 months and 8 days. The calculator uses the current date on the device, so its answer changes when the calendar date changes.

Worked examples

Birthday has already passed this year

Birth date: 15 February 2000. Comparison date: 20 November 2026. The birthday has passed in 2026, so the completed age is 26 years. From 15 February to 15 November is nine months, and from the 15th to the 20th is five days. The result is 26 years, 9 months, 5 days.

Birthday is still ahead this year

Birth date: 25 December 2000. Comparison date: 20 November 2026. The 2026 birthday has not happened yet, so the completed-year count is 25, not 26. Counting back from 25 December to 20 November gives 25 years, 10 months and 26 days using the calendar borrowing method.

End-of-month birthdays

Calendar arithmetic becomes less intuitive near the end of a month because months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days. When the current day number is less than the birth day number, the calculator borrows the number of days in the month immediately before the comparison date. This is why an answer may not match a rough mental estimate based on a fixed 30-day month.

Leap-day birthdays

A person born on 29 February has a birthday date that appears only in leap years. For the purpose of this calculator, a 29 February birth date is treated as 1 March in a non-leap year. That means the completed-year anniversary is recognised on 1 March when February has only 28 days. This convention is a practical rule for the calculator, but legal definitions of a birthday or age threshold can depend on the country and the specific law or institution involved.

Example: a leap-day birth date

Someone born on 29 February 2004 is compared with 28 February 2025. Under the calculator's rule, the 2025 birthday has not yet been reached because the non-leap-year anniversary is 1 March. On 1 March 2025, the person reaches the next completed year.

Age in days, weeks and months

Some situations require age in a single unit. A rough age in days can be estimated by counting the actual calendar days between the two dates, but the calculator's main output is a calendar age in years, months and days. Converting years into a fixed number of days can introduce an error because leap years add a day. Likewise, converting months to 30 days is only an approximation because calendar months are not all the same length.

If you need the exact number of days between two dates for a project or deadline, use the Date Calculator. If you need to count down to a future birthday or event, use the Countdown Calculator. Those are different tasks from calculating a person's calendar age.

Case study: checking an age requirement

Suppose a sports programme accepts participants who have turned 18 by 1 September. A participant was born on 2 September 2008. On 1 September 2026, the participant is still 17, even though the birthday is only one day away. On 2 September 2026, the participant turns 18. The age calculator can help explain the calendar difference, but the programme's published eligibility rules determine which date is legally or administratively relevant.

  2. A person was born on 1 January 2010. What is their age on 1 July 2026? Answer: 16 years, 6 months, 0 days.

Limitations and common mistakes

  • Wrong date format: check the date picker carefully before calculating.
  • Future birth date: an age cannot be calculated for a birth date later than today, so check the year and month.
  • Device date is wrong: the result uses the current date on the device. An incorrect device clock produces an incorrect age.
  • Legal age rules: legal adulthood, school admission cut-offs and competition eligibility may use special rules. The calculator does not interpret local law.
  • Time of birth: this tool works with calendar dates, not the exact hour, time zone or minute of birth.

Further questions about calendar age

Does the calculator update automatically?

It uses the current date when the calculation is performed. If you leave the page open across midnight, refresh or recalculate to use the new date.

Why is my age different from a days-divided-by-365 calculation?

Because calendar years and months have different lengths. The calculator reports calendar years, months and days rather than an average-year estimate.

Can I calculate the age of a child?

Yes. The arithmetic works for any valid past birth date, but it is not a developmental or medical assessment.

Can it calculate age at a past date?

The current tool compares the birth date with today's date. To calculate a historical interval, use a date-difference tool or an appropriate records system.

Age calculations for forms, school and travel

Age is often requested on school applications, sports registrations, travel documents and membership forms. These forms may ask for age on the date of application, age on a specified cut-off date, or simply a date of birth. The calculator gives a calendar age relative to today's date. If a form asks for age on another date, do not assume today's age is sufficient. Follow the organisation's instructions and use the relevant date for its eligibility rule.

Age is not always the same as elapsed time

Elapsed time can be measured in seconds or days between two exact moments. Calendar age is expressed using years, months and days. A year is not always 365 days, and a month is not a fixed number of days. As a result, the calendar expression is more suitable for ordinary age reporting, while an exact elapsed-time calculation may be needed for scientific experiments, contracts or service intervals.

Checking the birth date before you trust the result

When a result looks unexpected, check the original birth record rather than trying several nearby dates until one seems right. Date pickers can make it easy to select the wrong year, especially when scrolling through many years. Check the day, month and year together. Also confirm that the device date is correct. The calculator cannot know whether the date entered matches a birth certificate, passport or school record.

Time zones and time of birth

This tool compares calendar dates, not exact birth timestamps. Someone born close to midnight in one time zone may have a different calendar date recorded in another time zone. The age calculator does not adjust for birthplace, daylight-saving changes or the exact time of birth. For ordinary age in years, months and days, a recorded civil birth date is usually the appropriate input. For a legal or specialist calculation, follow the relevant authority's method.

When a calculated age is not an eligibility decision

An age result can help check arithmetic, but it cannot decide whether a person meets an institution's rules. Some competitions specify age on the first day of a season; some school systems use a fixed admission date; and laws may define when a person reaches a threshold in a particular way. The organisation responsible for the requirement has the final say. Use the calculator as a date arithmetic aid, then compare the result with the exact wording of the rule.

Keeping age records consistent

For official paperwork, use the date of birth exactly as it appears on the relevant identity or civil registration document. If two records disagree, resolve the discrepancy with the issuing authority rather than choosing whichever date produces the preferred age. For family history or personal records, it can also help to record the comparison date alongside the result. That makes the calculation reproducible later and avoids confusion when someone reads an old note after another birthday has passed.

Calendar age and birthdays in different months

People sometimes assume that every month contributes 30 days to an age calculation. That shortcut can be useful for a rough estimate, but it does not reproduce a calendar age. A period from 31 January to 28 February behaves differently from a period of 30 days, and February changes length in leap years. The calculator uses the calendar month lengths when borrowing days, which is why the exact result may differ from mental arithmetic based on 30-day months.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator account for leap years?

Yes. It works from real calendar dates, so leap days are included automatically.

Can I calculate my age on a past or future date?

Not on this page. It measures to today. To find the days between two chosen dates, use the Date Calculator.

Why does it show a dash?

The date of birth is empty or in the future. Choose a past date.

Is my date of birth stored?

Calculations run in your browser and are not stored by the calculator.
