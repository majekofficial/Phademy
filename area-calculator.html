Area of eleven common shape options, in the unit you choose

Pick a shape, choose the unit of length and enter the dimensions. The calculator returns the area in square units and, in the “more about this shape” panel, extra measurements such as perimeter, diagonal or circumference, together with the area converted to square metres and square feet. The available shape options are square, rectangle, triangle (base and height), triangle (three sides), circle, parallelogram, trapezium, rhombus or kite, ellipse, sector, ring and regular polygon.

The formulas

  • Square: a². Rectangle: l × w.
  • Triangle: ½ × base × height. With three sides, Heron's formula uses s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2 and area = √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)).
  • Circle: πr². Sector: (angle ÷ 360) × πr². Ring: π(R² − r²). Ellipse: π × a × b.
  • Parallelogram: base × height, where the height is measured at right angles to the base.
  • Trapezium: ½ × (a + b) × h, using the two parallel sides. Rhombus or kite: ½ × d₁ × d₂.
  • Regular polygon: n × s² ÷ (4 tan(180° ÷ n)), using n sides of length s.

Worked examples

Rectangles in different units

A room 8 m by 5 m has an area of 40 m², a perimeter of 26 m and a diagonal of 9.434 m. The panel also shows 430.5564 ft². If the same room is 12 ft by 10 ft the area is 120 ft².

A circle

A circular table with radius 7 cm: area = π × 7² = 153.938 cm². The circumference is 43.9823 cm and the diameter is 14 cm. Enter the radius, not the diameter. If you only know the diameter, halve it first.

A triangle from its sides

Sides 3, 4 and 5: s = 6, so the area is √(6 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 6 cm², with a perimeter of 12 cm. For base 10 and height 6 the triangle area is 30.

Other shapes

  • Parallelogram, base 9 and height 4: 36.
  • Trapezium with parallel sides 6 and 10 and height 4: ½ × 16 × 4 = 32.
  • Rhombus with diagonals 8 and 6: 24.
  • Ellipse with semi-axes 5 and 3: π × 15 = 47.1239.

Parts of circles

A quarter circle (sector with 90°) of radius 10 has area 78.5398, a quarter of π × 100. A ring with outer radius 10 and inner radius 6 has area π(100 − 36) = 201.0619. A regular hexagon with side 4 has area 41.5692.

Choosing the right unit

Always enter all lengths in the unit shown in the Units list, and read the result as square units of that length: 1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100. If your measurements are in different units, convert them first using the Unit Converter for kilometres, metres, feet and inches. For flooring, paint or tiling, add 5% to 10% for waste.

Common mistakes

  • Using the diameter where the radius is expected.
  • Measuring a slanted side instead of the perpendicular height of a triangle or parallelogram.
  • Forgetting that area is in square units, so converting from cm² to m² divides by 10,000.
  • Entering the sector angle in radians. The field expects degrees.

For three-dimensional solids, use the Volume Calculator. To work with exact square roots in a Heron's-formula answer, see the Surd Calculator. The Percentage Calculator helps with waste allowances.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the area of an irregular shape?

Split it into the simple shapes above, find each area and add them, subtracting any parts that are cut out.

What is the difference between area and perimeter?

Area measures the surface inside a shape in square units. Perimeter is the distance around its edge in ordinary units.

Why does a ring need two radii?

The area is the large circle minus the hole, π(R² − r²).

What is the height of a triangle?

The perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite corner, not the length of a slanted side.

How to choose the correct area formula

The most important step is identifying the shape and deciding which measurements the formula actually requires. A rectangle needs its full length and width. A triangle needs a base and the perpendicular height, or all three side lengths if you are using Heron's formula. A circle needs its radius. These are not interchangeable measurements. Before entering anything, sketch the object, label the known dimensions and mark any right angles. A quick sketch often prevents a more serious error than checking the arithmetic twice.

For composite shapes, do not search for one complicated formula straight away. Divide the outline into familiar pieces, calculate each piece and combine the results. If a floor plan looks like a rectangle with a smaller rectangle removed, calculate the large rectangle and subtract the missing section. If an L-shaped garden can be split into two rectangles, add their areas. Make sure the pieces do not overlap, otherwise you will count the overlapping region twice. This decomposition method is useful for rooms, plots, signs, walls and many classroom geometry questions.

Understanding the measurements for each shape

Square and rectangle

A square has four equal sides, so one side measurement is enough. Its area grows with the square of the side: doubling the side makes the area four times as large. A rectangle can have different length and width. Its area is the number of unit squares that fit inside it, which explains why multiplying the two perpendicular dimensions works. The calculator also reports perimeter and diagonal for these shapes. Perimeter is a length, while the diagonal is found using Pythagoras' theorem. Neither should be confused with the area.

Triangles

The base-and-height method uses the shortest perpendicular distance from the chosen base to the opposite vertex or its extended line. The height is not automatically the sloping side. An obtuse triangle may have a height that falls outside the triangle, but the same formula still works. When all three sides are known, Heron's formula is useful because a separate height does not have to be measured. First find the semi-perimeter, which is half the sum of the three sides. Then multiply the semi-perimeter by its differences from each side and take the square root. The side lengths must satisfy the triangle inequality: the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third.

Circle, sector and ring

The radius runs from the centre to the circumference. The diameter crosses the whole circle through the centre and is twice the radius. The area of a circle is proportional to the square of its radius, so a circle with twice the radius has four times the area. A sector is a slice of a circle. Its area is the same fraction of the full circle as its angle is of 360 degrees. A 90-degree sector therefore occupies one quarter of the circle. A ring, also called an annulus, is the region between two circles with the same centre. Subtract the inner circle's area from the outer circle's area. The inner radius must be smaller than the outer radius.

Parallelogram, trapezium and rhombus

For a parallelogram, the height is the perpendicular separation between the parallel bases. It is not the slanted side length. A trapezium uses the two parallel sides and the perpendicular height. The formula averages the parallel side lengths and multiplies by the height. A rhombus or kite can be calculated from its diagonals when the diagonals meet at right angles, as they do for these standard forms. The diagonal measurements are full diagonal lengths, not half-diagonals.

Ellipse and regular polygon

An ellipse has two semi-axes: the longer semi-axis and the shorter semi-axis, each measured from the centre to the edge. Do not enter the full major and minor diameters in those fields unless you first halve them. A regular polygon has equal sides and equal interior angles. The calculator asks for the number of sides and the length of one side. The side count must be a whole number from 3 to 100. An irregular polygon cannot be treated as regular simply because its outline looks roughly symmetrical.

Worked planning examples

Estimating flooring for a room

Imagine a rectangular room measuring 4.8 metres by 3.6 metres. Its floor area is 4.8 × 3.6 = 17.28 square metres. If you allow 8% for cuts and breakage, the extra amount is 17.28 × 0.08 = 1.3824 square metres. The practical purchase estimate is 18.6624 square metres, which you would round up according to the package size. The area calculator gives the base floor area; the waste allowance is a separate planning decision. For a room with a built-in cupboard that should not be tiled, calculate the cupboard footprint and subtract it before adding the waste margin.

Painting a wall

A wall 5 metres wide and 2.7 metres high has a gross area of 13.5 square metres. A door measuring 0.9 by 2.1 metres occupies 1.89 square metres, and a window measuring 1.2 by 1 metre occupies 1.2 square metres. The paintable area is 13.5 − 1.89 − 1.2 = 10.41 square metres. If two coats are needed, the coverage requirement is roughly 20.82 square-metre-coats. Paint tins state an estimated coverage, but surface texture and absorption affect the real amount. Use the calculator for the geometry, then follow the paint manufacturer's coverage guidance.

Finding a circular table top

A table top has a diameter of 1.4 metres. The radius is 0.7 metres, so its area is π × 0.7², approximately 1.539 square metres. If you enter 1.4 as the radius by mistake, the result becomes four times too large. If you are ordering a circular cloth, remember that the cloth needs to hang beyond the edge. That drop is not part of the table's top area and may require a different measurement based on the desired overhang.

Checking a triangular plot

A triangular plot has sides of 13 metres, 14 metres and 15 metres. The semi-perimeter is (13 + 14 + 15) ÷ 2 = 21 metres. Heron's formula gives the area as √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84 square metres. This is a useful check when the plot boundary can be measured but the height is difficult to access. If one of the side measurements is inaccurate, however, the calculated area will also be inaccurate. Surveying a real property boundary requires appropriate measurement and, where necessary, a qualified surveyor.

Units, scale and conversions

Area units are squared length units. A square metre is a square that is one metre on each side. Since one metre contains 100 centimetres, one square metre contains 100 × 100 = 10,000 square centimetres. This is why converting square centimetres to square metres requires division by 10,000, not by 100. Similarly, one square kilometre contains one million square metres. The calculator displays the area in the selected squared unit and also converts it to square metres and square feet. For sufficiently large areas, it also displays acres and hectares.

Keep all input dimensions in the same selected length unit. If a drawing gives one side in centimetres and another in metres, convert one before calculating. The Unit Converter can help with ordinary length conversions. Do not mix a measurement in feet with one in metres and then label the answer as square feet. For scale drawings, convert the drawing measurement to the real measurement first. If a plan uses a scale of 1:100, one centimetre on the plan represents one metre in reality, and the area scale factor is 100 squared, not merely 100.

Accuracy, rounding and practical limits

The result is only as reliable as the measurements entered. A wall that is not perfectly rectangular, a plot with an uneven boundary or a curved surface may need to be divided into more sections or measured with a more suitable method. The calculator rounds displayed values, while the underlying calculation may carry more precision. Keep unrounded values for intermediate steps and round only when you need a practical final quantity. For purchasing materials, follow package sizes and include a sensible allowance for waste. Do not treat a mathematical area estimate as a structural, surveying or construction specification.

For circles and sectors, π is an irrational number, so a decimal result is an approximation. For a regular polygon, the tangent in the formula is evaluated numerically. Small display differences between calculators can arise from rounding, even when the formulas are the same. A result such as 41.57 square units is not meaningfully more accurate than the measurements used to produce it if the side length was only measured to the nearest centimetre.

Common errors to check before trusting an answer

  • Entering a diameter into a radius field. Halve the diameter first.
  • Using a sloping edge as a triangle or parallelogram height. The height must be perpendicular to the base.
  • Entering semi-axis lengths as full ellipse diameters, or the reverse.
  • Forgetting to subtract openings such as doors and windows when estimating a paintable wall.
  • Adding overlapping pieces when splitting an irregular outline into simpler shapes.
  • Using a negative, zero or impossible measurement. The calculator requires positive dimensions and checks certain shape constraints.
  • Confusing square units with ordinary units. Area is measured in m², cm² or ft², not metres or feet.
  • Applying a length conversion factor only once to an area. Square the length conversion factor.

Frequently asked questions about area

Can I calculate the area of an irregular shape?

Not directly as one of the listed standard shapes. Divide it into rectangles, triangles or other familiar parts, calculate each part and add or subtract the results. For a highly irregular boundary, a survey or coordinate-based method may be more suitable.

Why does the calculator ask for a perpendicular height?

Area measures the space covered by a shape. The perpendicular height measures the shortest distance between parallel lines, which is the dimension required by the triangle, parallelogram and trapezium formulas.

Can area be negative?

No. A physical area is non-negative. The calculator asks for positive dimensions and rejects invalid shape inputs rather than returning a negative area.

What is the difference between area and surface area?

Area usually describes a flat two-dimensional region. Surface area describes the total area covering a three-dimensional object. Use the Volume Calculator when you need volume and the surface area of common solids.

How do I calculate the area of a room with an alcove?

Sketch the room as a large rectangle plus or minus smaller rectangular sections. Calculate each section separately, making sure there are no gaps or overlaps, and combine the results. If the room has angled walls, a triangle or trapezium may be a better component.
