Work done, power, kinetic energy, potential energy and efficiency

Energy is the capacity to do work, and power is how quickly the work is done. This calculator covers six relationships used in school physics: work done by a force, power from work and time, power from force and speed, kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy and efficiency.

The formulas

  • Work done: W = F × d, in joules (J), when the force acts along the direction of motion.
  • Power: P = W ÷ t, or P = F × v, in watts (W). One watt is one joule per second.
  • Kinetic energy: E = ½mv². Energy of motion, which grows with the square of speed.
  • Gravitational potential energy: E = mgh. Energy stored by height above a reference level.
  • Efficiency: η = (useful energy out ÷ total energy in) × 100.

Worked examples

Work and power

A 10 N force moves a box 5 m: W = 50 J. If a motor does 600 J of work in 30 s, its power is 600 ÷ 30 = 20 W. A 50 N force moving something at 4 m/s delivers 50 × 4 = 200 W.

Kinetic energy

A 2 kg ball at 3 m/s has E = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J. Doubling the speed would quadruple the energy, so at 6 m/s it would be 36 J.

Potential energy

A 5 kg object 10 m above the ground has E = 5 × 9.8 × 10 = 490 J. With g = 10 the figure is 500 J.

Efficiency

A device that takes in 200 J and gives out 40 J of useful energy is 40 ÷ 200 × 100 = 20% efficient. The other 160 J is wasted, usually as heat; the Heat Calculator shows how much energy it takes to warm a material by a given amount.

Putting it together: a dropped object

The 5 kg object falling from 10 m loses 490 J of potential energy. With no air resistance, all of it becomes kinetic energy: ½ × 5 × v² = 490, so v² = 196 and v = 14 m/s. The same answer comes from v² = 2gh in the Motion Calculator.

Why force and distance must line up

Work only counts the part of the force that acts along the motion. Carrying a bag horizontally at constant height does no work on it in the physics sense, because the upward support force is at right angles to the movement. This calculator assumes the force and distance are in the same direction.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to square the speed in kinetic energy.
  • Using mass in grams instead of kilograms.
  • Treating watts and joules as the same thing. Power is energy per unit time.
  • Quoting an efficiency above 100%, which would break conservation of energy.

Forces are explained in the Force Calculator, and the Simple Machines Calculator shows how machines trade force for distance. Electrical power has its own formulas in the Ohm's Law Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is a joule?

The energy transferred when a 1 N force moves an object 1 m.

What is the difference between energy and power?

Energy is the amount of work that can be done. Power is how fast energy is transferred.

Which value of g is used for potential energy?

You can choose 9.8, 10 or 9.81 m/s².

Can efficiency exceed 100%?

No. Some energy is always transferred to the surroundings, so useful output is always less than the input.

Work in physics is not the same as effort in everyday language

In physics, work is energy transferred when a force causes displacement. For a constant force acting in the direction of movement, W = Fd. Work is measured in joules, where one joule equals one newton metre. If a 10 N force moves an object 3 m along the force direction, the work done is 30 J. A person can feel tired while holding a heavy object stationary, but the force does no mechanical work on the object if there is no displacement.

The direction of force matters. If a force acts at an angle θ to the displacement, the more general expression is W = Fd cos θ. A force perpendicular to the displacement does zero work in this idealised calculation. The calculator's basic work mode uses force multiplied by distance along the direction of motion, so interpret the result using that assumption.

Positive, negative and zero work

Work is positive when a force has a component in the direction of displacement. Work is negative when the force component acts opposite the displacement. Friction often does negative work on a sliding object because it removes mechanical energy from the motion. Work is zero if there is no displacement or if the force is perpendicular to the displacement. These sign ideas help explain why work is a scalar quantity that can be positive, negative or zero.

For example, when a box slides to the right and friction acts to the left, friction does negative work. A person pulling the box to the right does positive work. The net work by all forces determines the change in kinetic energy. If the box moves at constant speed, the positive and negative work can balance over the interval.

Power measures the rate of energy transfer

Power tells you how quickly work is done or energy is transferred. Average power is P = W/t, measured in watts. One watt is one joule per second. Doing 600 J of work in 10 seconds gives an average power of 60 W. The same amount of work completed in 5 seconds requires an average power of 120 W. The energy transferred is the same, but the rate is different.

When a force acts in the direction of an object's velocity, instantaneous power can be written P = Fv. This is useful for moving vehicles and machines. If a motor exerts a driving force of 500 N while a vehicle moves at 10 m/s, the ideal mechanical power at that instant is 5,000 W or 5 kW. Real motors and drivetrains have losses, so input electrical or fuel power may be greater than useful output power.

Kinetic energy and why speed matters so much

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion: KE = ½mv². It depends on mass and the square of speed. Doubling the mass doubles kinetic energy at the same speed, while doubling speed makes kinetic energy four times as large. This square relationship helps explain why high-speed collisions can be much more severe than low-speed ones.

For example, a 1,000 kg car moving at 10 m/s has kinetic energy ½ × 1,000 × 10² = 50,000 J. At 20 m/s, its kinetic energy is 200,000 J. The speed has doubled, but the kinetic energy has quadrupled. Always square the speed before multiplying by half the mass, and make sure speed is in metres per second when using SI units.

Gravitational potential energy

Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy relative to a chosen reference level is GPE = mgh. Mass is in kilograms, gravitational field strength g in m/s² and height in metres. Raising a 2 kg object by 5 m using g = 9.8 m/s² increases its gravitational potential energy by 98 J. The height is a change relative to the reference level, not necessarily the object's total height above sea level.

The zero level for gravitational potential energy can be chosen for convenience. What matters in many problems is the change in potential energy between two positions. If an object moves down, its gravitational potential energy decreases. If air resistance is ignored, that lost potential energy can become kinetic energy. If friction or drag is present, some energy is transferred to thermal energy and sound.

Efficiency: useful output compared with input

Efficiency is the percentage of input energy or work that becomes useful output: η = (useful output / total input) × 100%. If a machine receives 500 J and transfers 400 J usefully, its efficiency is 80%. The remaining 100 J is not necessarily destroyed; it is transferred to less useful forms such as heat, sound or vibration.

Efficiency is not the same as power. A machine can be powerful but inefficient, or less powerful but efficient. Efficiency compares quantities of the same type, such as useful energy output with energy input. Do not divide energy by time unless you are calculating power. In a real system, efficiency cannot exceed 100% because useful output cannot be greater than total input when the system boundary and measurements are defined consistently.

Energy conservation and energy transfers

The principle of conservation of energy says that energy is not created or destroyed in an isolated system; it is transferred between stores or transformed from one form to another. A falling object loses gravitational potential energy and gains kinetic energy. A motor transfers electrical energy into mechanical energy, with some energy also transferred to the surroundings as heat and sound. A braking vehicle transfers kinetic energy mainly into thermal energy in the brakes and tyres.

When solving a problem, identify the starting and ending energy stores. If losses are ignored, the total mechanical energy may remain constant. If friction, drag or deformation is important, include the work done by non-conservative forces or account for the energy transferred to the surroundings. A calculator can evaluate the selected formula, but the physics model determines which energy terms belong in the equation.

Worked example: lifting and then releasing an object

A 2 kg object is lifted 4 m above a reference level. Using g = 9.8 m/s², its increase in gravitational potential energy is 2 × 9.8 × 4 = 78.4 J. If it is released from rest and air resistance is ignored, just before reaching the reference level it has gained 78.4 J of kinetic energy. Set ½mv² = 78.4. With m = 2 kg, v² = 78.4 and v ≈ 8.85 m/s.

This result follows from energy conservation and agrees with the constant-acceleration model for free fall. If air resistance is included, some of the initial potential energy is transferred to the air and the final speed is lower. This is a good example of using two different physics ideas to check the same situation.

Practice questions with answers

  1. A 25 N force moves an object 4 m along its direction. Find the work. Answer: W = 25 × 4 = 100 J.
  2. A motor transfers 900 J of energy in 15 s. Find average power. Answer: P = 900/15 = 60 W.
  3. A 3 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Find kinetic energy. Answer: KE = ½ × 3 × 4² = 24 J.
  4. A machine receives 800 J and provides 600 J of useful output. Find efficiency. Answer: 600/800 × 100 = 75%.

Units, assumptions and common interpretation errors

Use newtons for force, metres for distance or height, seconds for time, kilograms for mass, metres per second for speed, joules for energy and watts for power. One kilowatt is 1,000 watts, and one kilojoule is 1,000 joules. Convert before entering values if the question uses centimetres, grams, minutes or kilometres per hour. Keep the gravity value consistent with the problem statement.

Remember that work is energy transfer, energy is a quantity, power is a rate, and efficiency is a ratio expressed as a percentage. The calculator provides separate modes because the quantities answer different questions. Do not use a work formula when the question asks for power, and do not treat a machine's power rating as the amount of energy it uses without knowing the operating time.
