The slope of a line through two points

Slope, or gradient, measures how steeply a line rises or falls. Enter the coordinates of two points and the calculator returns the slope and a profile of the line: its equation, the intercepts, the angle with the x-axis, the distance between the points and their midpoint. Decimals and fractions such as 1/2 are accepted.

The formulas

slope m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

distance = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) | midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2)

The slope is the vertical change (rise) divided by the horizontal change (run). A positive slope rises to the right, a negative slope falls and a slope of 0 is a horizontal line.

Worked examples

A rising line

(1, 2) and (5, 10). Rise = 8, run = 4, so m = 2. The line is y = 2x, in standard form 2x − y = 0, and it passes through the origin. The angle with the x-axis is 63.4349°, the gradient as a percentage is 200%, the distance is 4√5 ≈ 8.9443 and the midpoint is (3, 6).

A falling line

(0, 4) and (6, 1). Rise = −3, run = 6, so m = −1/2 or −0.5. The equation is y = 4 − x/2, standard form x + 2y = 8. The y-intercept is (0, 4) and the x-intercept is (8, 0). The angle is −26.5651°, the distance 3√5 ≈ 6.7082 and the midpoint (3, 5/2).

A horizontal line

(−3, 2) and (3, 2). The rise is 0, so m = 0 and the line is y = 2. There is no x-intercept, and the distance is 6.

A vertical line

(2, 5) and (2, 9). The run is 0, so the slope is undefined and the line is x = 2. The angle is 90°, the distance is 4 and the midpoint is (2, 7).

Using the other results

  • Equation of the line: y = mx + c, with the intercept found from either point.
  • Gradient as a percentage: used for road and roof pitch. A 200% gradient rises 2 metres for every 1 metre across.
  • Angle with the x-axis: the inverse tangent of the slope. Use degrees when checking with the Scientific Calculator.
  • Distance and midpoint: useful in coordinate geometry and for finding the centre of a segment.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing the order of the points. You can use either point first, but be consistent in both numerator and denominator.
  • Subtracting x-values in one order and y-values in the other, which flips the sign.
  • Dividing by zero for a vertical line. The slope there is undefined, not zero.
  • Confusing slope with angle: a slope of 1 is an angle of 45°, not 1°.

What does a negative slope mean?

Why is the slope of a vertical line undefined?

What slope tells you about a line

Slope, also called gradient, describes the rate at which y changes as x changes. On a coordinate graph, it tells you how steep a straight line is and whether the line rises or falls as you move from left to right. A positive slope rises to the right, a negative slope falls to the right, and a zero slope is horizontal. A vertical line has no defined slope because its horizontal change is zero.

The slope is a ratio, not a distance. A line with slope 2 rises two vertical units for every one horizontal unit. A line with slope 1/2 rises one unit for every two horizontal units. These lines have different steepness, even though both rise as x increases. The sign gives the direction, while the absolute value gives the steepness. The actual units depend on the axes: if y is measured in metres and x in seconds, slope has units of metres per second.

Rise over run and the slope formula

Given two points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂), calculate the vertical change by subtracting y₁ from y₂. This is the rise. Calculate the horizontal change by subtracting x₁ from x₂. This is the run. Divide rise by run: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁). Always subtract in the same point order. If you reverse the points in both numerator and denominator, the two negative signs cancel and the slope remains the same.

For example, between (2,3) and (6,11), the rise is 11−3 = 8 and the run is 6−2 = 4. The slope is 8/4 = 2. If the points are entered in reverse order, the rise is 3−11 = −8 and the run is 2−6 = −4. The result is still 2. Mixing the order, such as using 11−3 over 2−6, creates an incorrect negative answer.

From slope to the equation of a line

Once the slope is known, a non-vertical line can be written in slope-intercept form: y = mx + c. Here m is the slope and c is the y-intercept, the value of y when x = 0. Substitute either known point into the equation and solve for c. If the slope is 2 and the line passes through (1,5), then 5 = 2×1 + c, so c = 3. The equation is y = 2x + 3.

The same line can be written in standard form Ax + By = C. Rearranging y = 2x + 3 gives 2x − y = −3. Different forms describe the same set of points. Slope-intercept form makes the gradient and y-intercept easy to see; standard form can be convenient when comparing equations or solving two lines together. A vertical line is different: it has an equation x = k, where k is constant, and cannot be written as y = mx + c.

Intercepts, angle and percentage gradient

The y-intercept is where the line crosses the vertical axis, so x = 0. In y = mx + c, it is (0,c). To find the x-intercept, set y = 0 and solve 0 = mx + c. When m is not zero, x = −c/m. A horizontal line y = c has no x-intercept unless c is zero; the line y = 0 lies on the x-axis and has infinitely many points in common with it.

The angle θ that a non-vertical line makes with the positive x-axis is related to slope by tan θ = m. Therefore θ = arctan(m). The calculator expresses the angle in degrees. A slope of 1 corresponds to 45 degrees, while a slope of 0 corresponds to 0 degrees. For a negative slope, the calculator may show a negative angle, indicating a clockwise direction from the positive x-axis. Do not confuse the numerical value of the slope with the angle itself.

Gradient as a percentage is the absolute rise divided by the run, multiplied by 100. A slope of 0.08 corresponds to an 8% gradient. A slope of 2 corresponds to 200%, not 2%. This percentage convention is useful for road gradients and ramps. A 100% gradient means the vertical rise equals the horizontal run; it does not mean a 100-degree angle.

Distance and midpoint are different from slope

The distance formula measures the straight-line separation between two points: d = √[(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²]. It comes from Pythagoras' theorem, because the horizontal and vertical changes form the perpendicular sides of a right triangle. For (1,2) and (4,6), the changes are 3 and 4, so the distance is √(9+16) = 5 units.

The midpoint is the point exactly halfway between the two endpoints. Average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates: ((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2). For (1,2) and (4,6), the midpoint is (2.5,4). Slope describes direction and steepness; distance describes length; midpoint describes location. They answer different questions, even though they use the same two points.

Special cases you must recognise

If x₁ = x₂ but y₁ and y₂ differ, the points form a vertical line. The run is zero, so division by zero is impossible and the slope is undefined. The line equation is x = x₁ and its angle is 90 degrees. The calculator identifies this case rather than reporting a very large number. If both x-coordinates and both y-coordinates are equal, the two inputs describe the same point. One point alone does not determine a unique line, so the calculator asks for two different points.

If y₁ = y₂ but the x-coordinates differ, the rise is zero and the slope is zero. The line is horizontal and has equation y = y₁. It has no single x-intercept unless it is the x-axis itself. These cases are common in coordinate geometry questions and are worth checking before using the general equation.

Worked examples with checks

Finding a line through two points

Take A(−2,1) and B(4,7). The rise is 6 and the run is 6, so m = 1. Substitute A into y = x + c: 1 = −2 + c, giving c = 3. The line is y = x + 3. The y-intercept is (0,3), and the x-intercept is (−3,0). Check point B: 7 = 4 + 3, so it lies on the line.

A negative gradient

Take (2,8) and (6,2). The rise is 2−8 = −6 and the run is 6−2 = 4, so m = −3/2. Using (2,8), calculate c = 8 − (−3/2×2) = 11. The equation is y = −1.5x + 11. As x increases by one, y decreases by 1.5. The negative sign is a direction indicator, not an error.

Common mistakes in coordinate geometry

  • Reversing only one subtraction: keep the same point order in the rise and run.
  • Calling a vertical slope zero: zero is the slope of a horizontal line; a vertical slope is undefined.
  • Confusing distance with slope: distance is non-negative length, while slope can be positive, negative, zero or undefined.
  • Using the wrong angle mode: the calculator reports degrees, so compare with degree-based trigonometry results.
  • Assuming the intercept is always positive: c can be negative or zero.
  • Forgetting units: if the axes use different physical units, the slope carries a compound unit.
  • Rounding too early: keep fractions such as 2/3 exact until the final decimal is needed.

How to use this slope calculator

Enter x₁, y₁, x₂ and y₂ for the two points. Whole numbers, decimals and fractions such as 1/2 are accepted. The calculator returns the slope, line equation, standard form, intercepts, angle, percentage gradient, distance, midpoint and a graph. Use the graph as a visual check, not as a replacement for the exact calculation. If the points are far apart or the coordinates are fractions, the graph may use a convenient viewing range.

To verify the line equation, substitute both original points into it. Both should satisfy the equation. To verify the midpoint, check that it is halfway in both coordinates. To verify distance, use the horizontal and vertical differences in Pythagoras' theorem. The Simultaneous Equations Calculator can find where two non-parallel lines meet, while the Surd Calculator helps simplify exact square roots in distance calculations.

Practice questions

  1. Find the slope between (1,4) and (5,12). Answer: (12−4)/(5−1) = 2.
  2. Find the equation through (0,−3) and (2,1). Answer: m = 2 and c = −3, so y = 2x−3.
  3. What is the slope of the line through (7,2) and (7,9)? Answer: undefined; it is vertical.
  4. Find the midpoint of (−2,5) and (4,−1). Answer: (1,2).
  5. Find the distance between (0,0) and (6,8). Answer: √(36+64) = 10 units.

Slope as a real-world rate

The gradient idea is not limited to drawing straight lines. If a graph shows distance vertically and time horizontally, its slope represents speed. A distance-time line that rises 100 metres over 20 seconds has slope 5 metres per second. If the graph shows temperature against time, slope represents the rate of temperature change. Always read the axis labels before interpreting the number, because the same numerical slope can represent different physical quantities depending on the units.

A steeper line has a greater absolute slope, but a negative slope is not automatically “slower” than a positive one. It indicates a decrease in y as x increases. For a graph of position against time, a negative gradient can mean motion in the negative direction. For a graph of account balance against time, it may mean the balance is falling. The context determines what the sign means.
