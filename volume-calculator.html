Calculate the volume of common three-dimensional shapes

Volume measures the amount of three-dimensional space occupied by a solid. It is used when estimating the capacity of a tank, the amount of material needed to fill a container, the space inside a box, or the size of a manufactured object. This volume calculator covers eight solids: cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere, hemisphere, rectangular pyramid and triangular prism. For most of these shapes it also reports surface area, and it converts the result into cubic metres, litres and cubic feet.

Choose the shape, select one length unit and enter the dimensions requested. Every measurement must be positive and expressed in the selected unit. The calculator does not automatically convert a radius in centimetres and a height in metres into a consistent pair, so convert mixed measurements first.

What volume means and why units are cubed

For a rectangular solid, volume is length multiplied by width multiplied by height. Each dimension measures one direction, so the product has three length factors. If each dimension is measured in centimetres, the result is in cubic centimetres, written cm³. Volume is not measured in square units because it describes three dimensions rather than a flat surface.

A cubic centimetre is the volume of a cube whose sides are each one centimetre long. A litre is equal to 1,000 cubic centimetres, and one cubic metre is equal to 1,000 litres. These relationships make it possible to compare the volume of a small object with the capacity of a larger container.

Cube volume and surface area

A cube has six equal square faces and all its edges have the same length. If the side length is a, the volume is V = a³. Its surface area is SA = 6a² because there are six faces, each with area a².

For a cube with side 3 cm, volume is 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³. Surface area is 6 × 3² = 54 cm². The volume in litres is 27 ÷ 1,000 = 0.027 L. Be careful not to confuse the cubic unit used for volume with the square unit used for surface area.

Cuboid volume and surface area

A cuboid, sometimes called a rectangular prism or box, has length l, width w and height h. Its volume is V = l × w × h. Its surface area is SA = 2(lw + lh + wh), because it has two faces of each of the three face dimensions.

For a box measuring 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm, volume is 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 cm³. Surface area is 2(5 × 4 + 5 × 3 + 4 × 3) = 2(20 + 15 + 12) = 94 cm². The box holds 60 mL because one cubic centimetre is equal to one millilitre.

Cylinder volume and surface area

A cylinder has two parallel circular ends and a curved side. Its volume is the area of the circular base multiplied by the perpendicular height: V = πr²h. Here r is the radius of the circular base and h is the distance between the two circular ends. The calculator also reports curved surface area, 2πrh, and total surface area, 2πr(r + h).

For a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, volume is π × 3² × 10 = 90π, approximately 282.7433 cm³. Its curved surface area is 2π × 3 × 10, approximately 188.4956 cm². Add the two circular ends, each with area 9π, to obtain total surface area of 78π, approximately 245.0442 cm². Its capacity is approximately 0.2827 litres.

Cone volume and surface area

A cone has a circular base that narrows to one point called the apex. Its volume is one third of the volume of a cylinder with the same base radius and perpendicular height: V = ⅓πr²h. The calculator also finds the slant height using the Pythagorean theorem, l = √(r² + h²), and uses it to calculate total surface area, πr(r + l).

For a cone with radius 3 cm and perpendicular height 10 cm, volume is ⅓ × π × 3² × 10 = 30π, approximately 94.2478 cm³. The slant height is √(3² + 10²) = √109, approximately 10.4403 cm. The total surface area is approximately 126.6719 cm².

Do not enter the slant height in the height field. Volume uses the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex. Slant height is the distance along the sloping surface from the edge of the base to the apex.

Sphere volume and surface area

A sphere is a perfectly round three-dimensional solid. Every point on its surface is the same distance from its centre. That distance is the radius r. The volume is V = ⁴⁄₃πr³, and the surface area is SA = 4πr².

For a sphere with radius 6 cm, volume is ⁴⁄₃ × π × 6³ = 288π, approximately 904.7787 cm³. Surface area is 4π × 6² = 144π, approximately 452.3893 cm². If the question gives a diameter, divide it by two before entering the radius.

Hemisphere volume and surface area

A hemisphere is half of a sphere, created by cutting a sphere through its centre. Its volume is V = ⅔πr³, exactly half the volume of the complete sphere. The calculator's total surface area includes the curved half-sphere and the flat circular base, giving SA = 3πr².

For radius 6 cm, hemisphere volume is 144π, approximately 452.3893 cm³. Its total surface area is 3π × 6² = 108π, approximately 339.2920 cm². If a question asks only for curved surface area, use 2πr² instead. Read carefully to determine whether the flat circular face is included.

Rectangular pyramid volume

A rectangular pyramid has a rectangular base and triangular side faces that meet at one apex. Its volume is one third of the base area multiplied by the perpendicular height: V = ⅓lwh. The base area is l × w, and h is the perpendicular distance from the base plane to the apex.

For a rectangular base measuring 6 cm by 4 cm and a perpendicular height of 9 cm, volume is ⅓ × 6 × 4 × 9 = 72 cm³. The calculator reports volume for this shape, but it does not display surface area for the rectangular pyramid. Surface area would require the slant heights of its triangular faces.

Triangular prism volume

A triangular prism has a triangular cross-section that continues unchanged along its length. The volume is the area of the triangular end multiplied by the prism length. Since the area of a triangle is one half times its base times its perpendicular height, the volume formula is V = ½ × b × t × L.

For a triangular cross-section with base 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm, extended for 10 cm, the volume is ½ × 6 × 4 × 10 = 120 cm³. The calculator asks for the triangle base, triangle height and prism length. It reports volume only for this shape, not its surface area.

Why a cone and pyramid have a one-third factor

A cone has one third the volume of a cylinder with the same circular base and perpendicular height. A pyramid has one third the volume of a prism with the same base area and perpendicular height. The factor of one third is a geometric result, not an adjustment chosen for convenience.

This relationship is useful as a memory aid. If you know the volume formula for a cylinder or prism, the corresponding cone or pyramid formula uses the same base area and height but includes a factor of one third. Do not apply that factor to a sphere, cube or cuboid.

Convert cubic units and litres

Length conversions are cubed when converting volume. Since 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 cm³ = 1,000,000 cm³. It is not 100 cm³ or 1,000 cm³. Since 1,000 cm³ = 1 L, one cubic metre contains 1,000 litres.

For a result of 60 cm³, divide by 1,000 to get 0.06 L. For 0.5 m³, multiply by 1,000 to get 500 L. The calculator shows the result in cubic metres, litres and cubic feet. If you need to convert the input dimensions before calculating, use the Unit Converter.

How to measure a real object

Choose the geometric model that best represents the object. A water bottle may be approximated as a cylinder, a storage box as a cuboid, and a ball as a sphere. Measure the dimensions required by the formula and record the unit beside each measurement. For containers, use internal dimensions if you need the usable capacity rather than the external volume.

Real objects may have rounded corners, thick walls, handles, openings or irregular shapes. A simple geometric calculation is an estimate when the object does not match the ideal solid exactly. For liquid capacity, remember that a container may not be safely filled to its brim, and the usable volume may be lower than the geometric volume.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Entering diameter instead of radius: radius is half the diameter.
  • Using slant height in a cone's volume formula: use perpendicular height for volume.
  • Mixing units: convert all dimensions to the selected unit before calculating.
  • Confusing square and cubic units: surface area uses units squared; volume uses units cubed.
  • Forgetting the one-third factor: cone and pyramid volume formulas include one third.
  • Assuming all surface areas are provided: this tool reports surface area for six of its eight solids, not for the rectangular pyramid or triangular prism.
  • Rounding dimensions too early: use accurate measurements and round the final result to a sensible precision.

What this calculator assumes and its limitations

The calculator assumes ideal geometric shapes and positive dimensions in a single selected unit. It calculates volume for all eight listed solids. It calculates surface area for cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere and hemisphere, but not for the rectangular pyramid or triangular prism. For those two shapes, a separate surface-area calculation is needed and may require additional side or slant measurements.

The calculator does not estimate material thickness, wall displacement, irregular cavities, manufacturing tolerances or the safe fill level of a container. For a real-world tank or container, compare the result with manufacturer specifications where safety or capacity limits matter. For mass, combine volume with density using the Density and Pressure Calculator. For a flat face, the Area Calculator can help calculate its area.

Estimate capacity without confusing it with external size

For a container, the dimensions that determine usable capacity are usually the internal dimensions. A tank with thick walls has an external volume larger than the space available for liquid. If you use external length, width and height, the calculated volume will overestimate the capacity. Measure inside the container where possible and account for internal supports, rounded corners or other features that take up space.

Capacity also depends on how the container is used. A tank may need free space at the top to prevent overflow, and a bottle may have a neck that narrows. The ideal solid formula estimates geometric volume; it does not determine the safe fill line. Follow manufacturer markings and safety guidance where applicable.

Accuracy, significant figures and measurement uncertainty

A calculator can display many decimal places, but the answer cannot be more accurate than the measurements. If a radius is measured only to the nearest centimetre, reporting a volume to ten decimal places suggests a precision the measurement does not support. Round the final result sensibly and use the significant-figure instructions provided by your course or project.

Small errors in a radius can have a noticeable effect on the volume of a sphere because the radius is raised to the third power. If a measurement is uncertain, record that uncertainty and avoid presenting the calculated capacity as an exact physical value. For engineering, construction or laboratory work, use the measurement and tolerance standards relevant to the task.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between volume and surface area?

Volume measures the space inside a solid and uses cubic units. Surface area measures the total area of its outside faces and uses square units.

How many litres are in one cubic metre?

One cubic metre is equal to 1,000 litres.

How do I calculate the volume of a pipe?

Model the inside of the pipe as a cylinder. Use the internal radius and the pipe's length in V = πr²h.

Should I enter the radius or diameter?

Enter the radius. If you only know the diameter, divide it by two first.

Why is a cone one third of a cylinder?

A cone and a cylinder with the same circular base and perpendicular height have volumes in a fixed ratio of one to three.

Why does the calculator not show every surface area?

It currently calculates surface area for the cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere and hemisphere. It reports volume only for the rectangular pyramid and triangular prism.

Can I use different units for different dimensions?

Not directly. Convert all dimensions to one unit first, then select that unit in the calculator.
