What the date calculator measures

Dates appear in project schedules, school calendars, rental agreements, travel plans, contracts and personal milestones. It is often useful to know the exact number of calendar days between two dates. This calculator takes a start date and an end date, then returns the absolute difference in days. You can enter the earlier date first or the later date first; the result is shown as a positive number either way.

The result is a calendar-day difference. It is not automatically a count of working days, weeks of employment, billable days or nights spent away. Those tasks may use different counting rules. Before using a result in a form or agreement, check whether the instructions count the first day, the last day, or both.

The formula and counting rule

days between = absolute value of (end date − start date)

Computers represent dates as points on a timeline. The calculator converts each selected calendar date to midnight on that date, finds the difference, and expresses it in whole 24-hour days. Because it uses calendar dates rather than the time of day, the difference is not affected by the hour at which you open the page.

The start date is not counted as a completed day. If you choose 1 June and 2 June, the dates are one day apart. The elapsed interval runs from the beginning of 1 June to the beginning of 2 June. This is called an exclusive-start difference. If a task asks you to count both the first and final dates, add one to the result.

Worked examples

Two consecutive dates

Start: 14 April. End: 15 April.

15 April − 14 April = 1 day

The dates are one day apart even though two date labels appear on a calendar.

A period covering most of a year

From 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2025 is 364 days. The start date is not counted as a completed day. If you are counting every calendar date from 1 January through 31 December inclusively, there are 365 dates.

A leap year

From 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2024 is 365 days. The year 2024 includes 29 February, so the interval is one day longer than the corresponding interval in a common year.

Crossing February

From 28 February to 1 March is 1 day in 2023, because the next date is 1 March. In 2024 it is 2 days: 29 February falls between the two dates.

Converting a result into weeks and days

From 1 March 2026 to 25 December 2026 is 299 days. Divide by 7: 299 ÷ 7 = 42 weeks and 5 days. This conversion is useful for planning, but it does not turn the interval into 42 working weeks.

Entering dates in reverse order

If the first field is 25 December and the second is 1 March of the same year, the calculator still reports the positive difference. It does not label the interval as negative or infer which date is the start of a project.

Inclusive and exclusive counting

The most common source of confusion is whether the first day is included. In an ordinary date subtraction, the start date is the boundary, not a counted full day. For example, a three-day interval from Monday to Thursday has three elapsed days: Monday to Tuesday, Tuesday to Wednesday and Wednesday to Thursday.

Some practical situations count calendar dates touched rather than elapsed 24-hour periods. If a person takes leave from Monday through Friday and both Monday and Friday are included, that is five leave dates even though Friday minus Monday is four days. Add one when both endpoints are to be counted and the dates are in the intended order. Do not add one automatically to every calculation; follow the wording of the task.

In legal, financial or employment contexts, the counting convention may be set by a contract, regulation or organisation. A calculator cannot decide that convention. Confirm the rule before using the result to determine a filing deadline, notice period or payment.

Calendar days, working days and business days

Calendar days include every date: weekdays, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. Working-day calculations usually exclude weekends and may also exclude public holidays. The number of working days depends on the country, employer, school or organisation's calendar.

A rough estimate for a long interval is five weekdays per full week, but this is not an exact business-day count. It can be wrong when the interval begins or ends midweek, crosses a public holiday, or follows a work schedule other than Monday to Friday. This calculator does not have a holiday calendar and does not remove weekends.

For a more precise work schedule, list the relevant dates and exclude the non-working days according to the applicable calendar. A shift worker may have a repeating rota, so a simple Monday-to-Friday assumption may not apply.

Months and years are not fixed day counts

It is tempting to convert every month to 30 days or every year to 365 days. That shortcut can create errors. Calendar months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days, and leap years contain an extra day. A period from 1 February to 1 March is not always the same length as a period from 1 March to 1 April.

For this reason, the calculator reports days rather than a rounded number of months. If you need a person's age expressed in years, months and days, use the Age Calculator. If you need to plan a countdown to a future date, try the Countdown Calculator.

Using date differences in everyday planning

  • Assignments and projects. Calculate the calendar interval between the start and due date, then set smaller milestones before the deadline.
  • Travel. Count the dates between departure and return, but remember that hotels may charge by nights rather than by date difference alone.
  • Rentals. Check whether the agreement counts the start and end dates and whether a partial day is treated as a full day.
  • Subscriptions. Renewal periods may follow calendar months rather than a fixed number of days. Read the provider's terms.
  • Personal goals. A day count can help track a habit or preparation period, but a streak may use a different rule for the first day.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting both endpoints by default. Date subtraction does not count the start date as a completed day. Add one only for an inclusive count.
  • Confusing date formats. 04/05 may mean 4 May or April 5 depending on the format. Use the date picker and check the selected year.
  • Assuming business days. Weekends and holidays remain included in the result.
  • Replacing months with 30 days. Calendar months have different lengths.
  • Forgetting leap day. A leap year changes the number of days in intervals that cross 29 February.
  • Mixing dates and clock times. This tool compares calendar dates, not the exact number of hours between two timestamps.
  • Using a day count as a legal interpretation. A contract or official rule may define its own counting method.

What the calculator can and cannot tell you

Choose a start date and end date. The calculator compares the two dates and displays the absolute difference in days. It handles ordinary calendar changes, including leap days, through the date system used by the browser. Reversing the order of the dates does not change the positive result.

It does not calculate business days, public holidays, nights, months of service, exact working hours or a person's age. It also does not interpret a deadline rule. For an elapsed time between clock times, use the Time Duration Calculator. For a longer savings plan, the Savings Goal Calculator can help translate a target into regular contributions.

Practice questions

  1. How many days are between 10 May and 20 May in the same year?
  2. How many days are between 1 January 2024 and 1 January 2025?
  3. A five-day event runs from Monday through Friday. What is the date difference from Monday to Friday, and how many dates are included if both days count?
  4. How many days are between 28 February 2024 and 2 March 2024?

Answers: 1. 10 days. 2. 366 days because 2024 is a leap year. 3. Four elapsed days, but five inclusive calendar dates. 4. Three days: 29 February, 1 March and the interval to 2 March.

Planning a deadline with a day count

A date difference is useful as a planning input, but it is not a complete schedule. If a project begins on 3 September and is due on 18 September, the date difference is 15 days. That does not mean every one of those days is available for work. You may have classes, weekends, public holidays or other commitments during the interval. Convert the total into smaller milestones and identify which days are actually usable.

For a submission deadline, also confirm the exact cut-off time. A date-only calculation cannot tell you whether a portal closes at noon, 5:00 pm or midnight. If the deadline is in a different time zone, confirm the organiser's stated time zone before making a plan. A one-day difference can matter when an application or payment must arrive before a fixed cut-off.

Dates, durations and anniversaries are different questions

“How many days between these dates?”, “How many months have I been here?” and “When is the next anniversary?” may sound similar, but they require different calculations. A day difference measures the elapsed calendar interval. A month-based period follows calendar month boundaries, which vary in length. An anniversary usually refers to the same month and day in a later year, with special handling needed for dates such as 29 February.

For example, 31 January to 28 February is 28 days in a common year, but it is not a full 30-day month. If a contract defines a period in months, do not replace that wording with a fixed number of days unless the contract or relevant rule says to do so. The Age Calculator is designed for age in years, months and days; this tool is designed for the difference between two selected dates.

How to record a date interval clearly

When sharing a date calculation with another person, write the full dates rather than only the number of days. For example, record “14 April 2026 to 29 April 2026: 15 elapsed calendar days” instead of “15 days”. This makes the endpoints and the counting method clear. If the result is for an inclusive form, state that both endpoints have been counted. Clear wording helps prevent a correct calculation from being misunderstood.

For recurring schedules, do not assume every interval has the same length. A monthly reminder on the 31st may not have a matching date in every month. Calendar applications and organisations may move such events to the final day of a shorter month or apply another rule. Check the system's behaviour rather than treating a month as a fixed number of days.

Keep a copy of the original dates when the calculation is important. If a date is changed later, the result will change too. A written record makes it easier to explain how the number was obtained and to repeat the calculation consistently.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator include the start date?

No. It calculates the elapsed difference between the two date boundaries. Add one if the task explicitly requires both endpoints to be counted.

Does it include leap years?

Yes. A leap day is included when it falls between the selected dates.

Can I enter the end date first?

Yes. The calculator uses the absolute difference and returns a positive number of days.

Are Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays excluded?

No. The result is a calendar-day count. It does not use a business-day or public-holiday calendar.

Why is a year sometimes 365 days and sometimes 366?

A leap year has 366 calendar days because February has 29 days. A common year has 365.

Can I use it to calculate hotel nights?

The date difference can give the number of nights between check-in and check-out dates in many ordinary bookings, but always follow the accommodation's booking rules and check the actual dates.
