Frequency, wavelength, wave speed and period

Every wave, from sound to light to ripples on water, links four quantities: its speed, frequency, wavelength and period. This calculator uses the wave equation v = fλ and the relationship between frequency and period.

The formulas

v = f × λ

f = 1 ÷ T and T = 1 ÷ f

  • Wave speed v in m/s: how fast the wave pattern travels.
  • Frequency f in hertz (Hz): the number of complete waves passing a point per second.
  • Wavelength λ in metres: the distance between two matching points, such as crest to crest.
  • Period T in seconds: the time for one complete wave.

Applying the wave formula in examples

Wave speed

A wave with frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m travels at 5 × 2 = 10 m/s.

Frequency from period

An oscillation with a period of 0.02 s has a frequency of 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50 Hz. This is the frequency of mains electricity in many countries.

Sound in air

Sound travels at about 340 m/s in air. A note with wavelength 0.5 m has frequency 340 ÷ 0.5 = 680 Hz. In reverse, a 680 Hz note travelling at 340 m/s has wavelength 340 ÷ 680 = 0.5 m.

What changes and what stays the same

  • The speed of a wave depends on the medium. When a wave moves from one medium to another, its frequency stays the same, so the wavelength changes in proportion to the speed.
  • At a fixed speed, doubling the frequency halves the wavelength.
  • For light and other electromagnetic waves in a vacuum, v is about 3 × 10⁸ m/s. A radio station at 100 MHz (1 × 10⁸ Hz) has a wavelength of 3 m.
  • Higher-frequency sound is heard as a higher pitch, and greater amplitude as a louder sound.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units, such as kilohertz with hertz or centimetres with metres.
  • Using the period in the frequency box, or the other way round.
  • Assuming the frequency changes when a wave enters a new medium. It does not; the wavelength does.
  • Confusing amplitude with wavelength. Amplitude is the height of the wave, wavelength is its length.

Frequently asked questions

What is the unit of frequency?

The hertz (Hz), which means one cycle per second.

What does the wave equation say?

Wave speed equals frequency times wavelength.

Is the period the same as the wavelength?

No. The period is a time in seconds and the wavelength is a distance in metres.

Why is frequency unchanged when a wave changes medium?

The source sets the frequency. The medium changes the speed and therefore the wavelength.

Understanding the wave equation

A wave transfers energy from one place to another without requiring the material of the medium to travel with the wave over the same distance. Water particles move around their positions as a ripple passes, and air particles vibrate as sound travels. The pattern moves through the medium. The wave equation connects wave speed, frequency and wavelength: v = fλ. Wave speed v is measured in metres per second, frequency f in hertz, and wavelength λ in metres.

Frequency describes how many complete cycles pass a fixed point each second. One hertz means one cycle per second. A frequency of 50 Hz means fifty complete cycles pass a point every second. Wavelength is the distance between two consecutive points that are in the same phase, such as crest to crest or compression to compression. Wave speed is the distance travelled by the wave pattern per second.

The equation is a rate relationship. If a wave has a frequency of 4 Hz and a wavelength of 3 m, then v = 4×3 = 12 m/s. If speed and wavelength are known, rearrange to f = v/λ. If speed and frequency are known, rearrange to λ = v/f. The current calculator's wave-speed mode solves v from frequency and wavelength; its frequency-and-period mode uses the reciprocal relationship described below.

Frequency and period

The period T is the time taken for one complete cycle. It is measured in seconds. Frequency and period are reciprocals: f = 1/T and T = 1/f. A wave with a period of 0.02 seconds has frequency 1/0.02 = 50 Hz. A wave with frequency 5 Hz has a period of 1/5 = 0.2 seconds. A high frequency means each cycle takes less time, while a low frequency means each cycle takes longer.

Do not confuse period with wavelength. Period is a time measurement, while wavelength is a distance measurement. They are connected through wave speed: v = λ/T, because the wave travels one wavelength during one period. Combining f = 1/T with v = fλ gives the same relationship. This is a useful way to understand the formula rather than memorising it as an isolated equation.

Wave calculation examples

Calculating wave speed

A ripple has a frequency of 6 Hz and wavelength 0.5 m. The speed is v = 6×0.5 = 3 m/s. This means the wave pattern moves three metres in one second. If the frequency doubles to 12 Hz while the medium and wave speed remain unchanged, the wavelength becomes 0.25 m. Frequency and wavelength change inversely when speed is fixed.

Finding frequency from period

A vibrating object completes one cycle in 0.004 seconds. The frequency is f = 1/0.004 = 250 Hz. The short period corresponds to many cycles each second. If a result seems surprisingly high or low, check whether the period was entered in seconds. A period of 4 milliseconds is 0.004 seconds, not 4 seconds.

Sound waves

Suppose sound travels through air at approximately 340 m/s and has a wavelength of 0.68 m. Its frequency is f = v/λ = 340/0.68 = 500 Hz. This is a mid-range audible tone. The speed of sound depends on the medium and conditions such as temperature, so 340 m/s is an approximate example rather than a universal constant. Use the speed appropriate to the question.

Radio waves

Electromagnetic waves in a vacuum travel at approximately 3.0×10⁸ m/s. A radio signal with frequency 100 MHz has frequency 100,000,000 Hz. Its wavelength is about 3.0×10⁸/1.0×10⁸ = 3 m. Converting megahertz to hertz before using the equation prevents a factor-of-one-million error. The calculator uses the units shown in its fields, so make sure the values you enter are in the expected base units.

What happens when a wave enters a different medium?

When a wave crosses a boundary into another medium, its speed may change. The frequency remains set by the source, so it stays the same across the boundary. Since v = fλ and f is unchanged, the wavelength changes in proportion to the speed. If the speed becomes smaller, the wavelength becomes shorter. If the speed becomes larger, the wavelength becomes longer.

This principle is important for refraction. Light changes speed and wavelength when it enters glass or water, while its frequency remains the same. The direction can also change if the wave meets the boundary at an angle. The calculator's simple equation calculates the relationship between the numerical quantities; it does not trace a ray through several media or calculate the angle of refraction.

Transverse and longitudinal waves

In a transverse wave, the oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of travel. A wave on a stretched string is a familiar example: the string moves up and down while the wave travels along it. In a longitudinal wave, the oscillation is parallel to the direction of travel. Sound in air is longitudinal, with compressions and rarefactions moving through the air. Wavelength can be measured between adjacent crests for a transverse wave or adjacent compressions for a longitudinal wave.

Amplitude is another wave property, but it is not one of the quantities used by this calculator. Amplitude measures the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position. For sound, greater amplitude is associated with greater intensity or loudness, while frequency is associated with pitch. For a simple sinusoidal wave, amplitude does not appear in v = fλ. Do not substitute amplitude for wavelength.

Units and scientific notation

Use consistent units. Frequency should be in hertz, wavelength in metres, period in seconds and speed in metres per second. Convert kilohertz to hertz by multiplying by 1,000, and convert centimetres to metres by dividing by 100. For example, 25 cm is 0.25 m. If you enter 25 as though it were metres, the calculated speed will be one hundred times too large.

Very large and very small values are often written in scientific notation. For example, 3×10⁸ means 300,000,000, and 2.5×10⁻³ means 0.0025. Scientific notation makes it easier to track powers of ten in radio, light and microscopic-wave problems. Keep the exponent when multiplying or dividing; do not treat 10⁸ as the number 10 multiplied by 8.

Common mistakes in wave calculations

  • Mixing units: convert centimetres to metres and kilohertz or megahertz to hertz before substituting.
  • Using period as frequency: they are reciprocals, not interchangeable values.
  • Confusing amplitude and wavelength: amplitude is displacement; wavelength is a distance between matching points.
  • Assuming frequency changes at a boundary: for a steady source, frequency remains the same while speed and wavelength may change.
  • Using an inappropriate wave speed: sound speed varies by medium and conditions; electromagnetic waves in vacuum use a different speed.
  • Forgetting the unit in the final answer: speed should be m/s, frequency Hz, period s and wavelength m.
  • Rounding too early: retain enough significant figures during intermediate steps.

Wave measurements and experimental care

In a practical investigation, frequency can be measured by counting how many complete oscillations occur in a known time. If 20 oscillations take 4 seconds, the frequency is 20/4 = 5 Hz. Measuring several cycles is often more reliable than timing a single cycle because reaction-time error has a smaller effect on the average period. The period is then T = 1/f = 0.2 s.

Wavelength can be estimated by measuring the distance between matching points on adjacent cycles, such as crest to crest. Do not measure from a crest to the next trough, because that is half a wavelength. If the wave is travelling, mark a fixed point and observe how often a crest passes it to estimate frequency. Use the same medium and conditions during a comparison, because changing the medium can change wave speed and wavelength while the source frequency remains fixed.

Practice questions

  1. A wave has frequency 8 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find its speed. Answer: 16 m/s.
  2. A wave travels at 300 m/s with wavelength 1.5 m. Find its frequency. Answer: 200 Hz.
  3. A source vibrates 20 times in 4 seconds. Find frequency and period. Answer: 5 Hz and 0.2 s.
  4. A wave has period 0.005 s. Find its frequency. Answer: 200 Hz.
  5. A wave enters a slower medium. What happens to its frequency and wavelength? Answer: frequency stays the same; wavelength decreases as speed decreases.

Frequency, pitch and the limits of a simple model

For a steady periodic sound, a higher frequency is perceived as a higher pitch. This does not mean a higher frequency must travel faster through the same air. In a given medium under the same conditions, sound waves of different frequencies travel at approximately the same speed, so their wavelengths differ. A 1,000 Hz tone has a shorter wavelength than a 500 Hz tone in the same air because the frequency has doubled while the speed is approximately unchanged.

Real waves can be more complicated than the ideal relationships used in introductory questions. A sound may contain many frequencies at once, and a pulse may not have one single wavelength. The calculator assumes that the quantities describe a regular wave with a meaningful frequency and wavelength. It does not analyse an audio recording or separate a complex sound into its component frequencies. For a school calculation, identify the single wave being described and use the supplied values consistently.
