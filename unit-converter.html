Converting between metric and imperial measurements

This converter handles the eight conversions people reach for most often in daily life, in both directions: kilometres and miles, kilograms and pounds, metres and feet, centimetres and inches. Pick the conversion, type a value and the result appears with up to six decimal places.

The conversion factors

  • 1 kilometre = 0.621371 miles, and 1 mile = 1.609344 kilometres
  • 1 kilogram = 2.204623 pounds, and 1 pound = 0.453592 kilograms
  • 1 metre = 3.28084 feet, and 1 foot = 0.3048 metres
  • 1 centimetre = 0.393701 inches, and 1 inch = 2.54 centimetres

Each result is the value multiplied by the factor for the direction you choose. To convert in your head, a rough rule is that a kilometre is about 0.6 of a mile, a kilogram is a little over 2.2 pounds and an inch is 2.5 centimetres.

Worked examples

Running distance

A 5 km run is 5 × 0.621371 = 3.106855 miles. A marathon of 26.2 miles is 26.2 × 1.609344 = 42.164813 km.

Body and luggage weight

10 kg is 22.046226 pounds. A 154 lb person weighs about 69.853225 kg, which you can use in the BMI Calculator.

Height

6 feet is exactly 1.8288 metres. 100 metres is 328.08399 feet. For small lengths, 30 cm is 11.811024 inches and 72 inches is 182.88 cm.

Getting accurate results

  • Choose the direction carefully. “Kilometers → Miles” and “Miles → Kilometers” are separate options, so check the arrow before you type.
  • Round to a sensible precision. 3.106855 miles is better written as 3.1 miles for a run, but keep all digits if the result feeds another calculation.
  • Feet and inches are separate units. For 5 ft 9 in, convert 5 feet and 9 inches separately, or work out 5 × 12 + 9 = 69 inches first, then convert 69 inches to centimetres (175.26).

What this converter does not cover

It does not include temperature, volume, area, speed or time. For speed and distance problems that mix units, the Speed, Distance and Time Calculator accepts kilometres and hours, metres and seconds, or miles and hours directly. The Area Calculator and Volume Calculator let you choose the length unit for each shape, so you do not have to convert first.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying when you should divide, and the reverse. A mile is longer than a kilometre, so a distance in miles is a smaller number than the same distance in kilometres.
  • Converting squared or cubed units with the linear factor. 1 square metre is not 3.28 square feet; it is about 10.76 square feet, because the factor is squared.
  • Using rounded factors in long calculations, which compounds the error.

To work out percentage changes between before and after values, try the Percentage Calculator. For money rather than measurements, use the Currency Converter.

Choosing the correct conversion

A unit conversion expresses the same physical quantity using a different unit. It does not change the underlying length, mass or other measurement. For example, 1 kilometre and 1,000 metres describe the same distance. The converter works by multiplying the value you enter by a fixed conversion factor for the selected direction. Choose the pair that matches both the unit you have and the unit you want.

Worked examples by measurement type

Distance: kilometres to miles

A journey is 12 km. Multiply 12 by 0.621371. The result is 7.456452 miles, displayed as 7.456452. The distance has not become shorter; it is simply being expressed in a unit with a different size.

Distance: miles to kilometres

A road sign shows 5 miles. Multiply 5 by 1.609344 to get 8.04672 km. This is useful when comparing a route measured in miles with a map or travel plan that uses kilometres.

Mass: kilograms to pounds

A parcel has a mass of 3 kg. Multiply 3 by 2.2046226218 to get approximately 6.613868 lb. A shipping company may round or apply its own billing increments, so use its rules when calculating postage.

Length: centimetres to inches

A screen measurement is 30 cm. Multiply 30 by 0.3937007874 to get approximately 11.811024 inches. This conversion is useful when product specifications use different measurement systems.

Why reverse conversions use different factors

Conversion factors in opposite directions are reciprocals. One kilometre is approximately 0.621371 miles, while one mile is exactly 1.609344 kilometres. The numbers are not identical because the units have different sizes. Multiplying by a factor converts from the first unit to the second; dividing by that factor would reverse the operation. The tool provides separate direction choices to reduce the chance of applying the wrong operation.

Case study: checking a travel distance

A traveller is planning a 140-mile drive but is used to reading distances in kilometres. Selecting miles to kilometres and entering 140 gives 225.30816 km. They can use that estimate to compare the journey with a kilometre-based route planner. The result is a unit conversion only: it does not account for traffic, road closures, route changes or the actual distance driven.

Dimensional checks and sensible rounding

A useful way to catch mistakes is to check whether the answer is in the expected direction. A kilometre is longer than a mile, so the numerical value in miles should be smaller than the same distance in kilometres. A pound is lighter than a kilogram, so a mass expressed in pounds should have a larger number than the same mass in kilograms. If the answer moves in the opposite direction, check the conversion direction and factor.

Do not report more decimal places than the original measurement justifies. If a tape measure gives a length to the nearest centimetre, a converted answer with six decimal places suggests more certainty than the measurement contains. Round to a sensible level for the task, while keeping the calculator's unrounded conversion available for further work when needed.

Limitations and common mistakes

  • Choosing the wrong direction: check whether your starting value is in the first or second unit.
  • Confusing mass and weight: kilograms and pounds are commonly used for everyday body or parcel mass, although pound-force is a different physical quantity.
  • Assuming every unit is supported: this converter covers the listed pairs only. It does not currently convert temperature, area, volume, speed or every possible unit.
  • Rounding too early: keep intermediate values unrounded if you are doing a multi-step calculation.
  • Ignoring context: shipping, construction and laboratory work may require prescribed rounding or measurement standards.

Accuracy, precision and significant figures

Accuracy describes how close a measurement is to the true value; precision describes the detail or repeatability of the measurement. A converter can apply a precise factor, but it cannot improve an imprecise original measurement. If a person measures a table as “about 2 metres”, converting it to 6.56168 feet does not make the original measurement accurate to five decimal places. The extra digits are a mathematical consequence of the factor, not new information about the table.

Choose rounding for the job

For a quick travel estimate, one or two decimal places may be enough. For a school exercise, follow the significant-figure instructions in the question. For manufacturing or laboratory work, use the tolerance and measurement standard specified by the task. Keep more digits during intermediate calculations and round the final result at the end, unless the instructions say otherwise.

Check whether the quantity is the same

Unit conversion is valid only when the units describe the same kind of quantity. Kilometres and miles are both units of length; kilograms and pounds are commonly used as units of mass in everyday contexts; centimetres and inches are units of length. Do not convert a mass into a force or a length into an area by applying one of these factors. Different physical quantities require different formulas and, sometimes, additional information.

Practical situations where conversions help

Unit conversions are common when reading international product specifications, comparing road distances, following exercise plans, weighing parcels and using equipment designed in another measurement system. A recipe may use grams while a scale displays ounces; a travel guide may give miles while a vehicle dashboard shows kilometres. The conversion makes the units comparable, but the user still needs to check any domain-specific rule such as shipping weight bands or a manufacturer's stated tolerance.

Why a conversion may not match a label exactly

Manufacturers often round dimensions for marketing or packaging. A product labelled as 12 inches may be described as 30 cm, even though 12 inches converts to 30.48 cm. This is not necessarily a calculator error: the label may be rounded to a convenient nominal size. If a close fit matters, use the detailed specification or measure the item directly rather than relying on a rounded product name.

Use the conversion as a check, not a substitute for measurement

When dimensions affect safety or fit, verify them against the original specification or measure the object directly. A converted value cannot correct a faulty tape measure, a rounded catalogue description or a measurement taken from the wrong reference point. For classroom work, show the factor and the unit cancellation in your working. For practical work, note the source measurement, the converted result and the rounding you applied so another person can reproduce the calculation.

Interpreting a converted measurement in context

Before using a converted value, check whether the source is a nominal size, an exact standard or a measured value. A nominal pipe size, clothing size or screen size may not equal a directly measured dimension. The conversion factor can still be mathematically correct while the interpretation of the label is wrong. Read the product or engineering specification to find out what the number represents, and convert the actual dimension when that is what the task requires.

Conversion factors are directional

Always read a factor as a statement about one unit. For example, 1 mile equals 1.609344 kilometres. The factor 1.609344 therefore belongs to the miles-to-kilometres direction. For kilometres-to-miles, use the reciprocal relationship, approximately 0.621371. Writing the unit beside each number during a school calculation helps the units cancel in the correct direction and makes a reversed conversion easier to spot.

Record the original unit with the result

When you copy a converted value into a report, include both the number and its unit. Writing “8.05” without saying kilometres or miles can create a serious misunderstanding. A clear record such as “5 miles ≈ 8.05 kilometres” preserves the direction of the conversion and lets another reader check the calculation. This small habit is useful in homework, travel notes, product comparisons and technical documentation.
