Converting an amount with an exchange rate you enter

This converter multiplies an amount by the exchange rate you provide. It does not look up live market rates, which has a practical advantage: you can use the exact rate shown by your bank, a money-transfer service or a shop, and see the figure you will actually receive.

How to enter the rate

The rate means how many units of the currency you are buying equal one unit of the currency you have. If 1 dollar equals 0.92 euros, the rate is 0.92 and the result is in euros.

converted amount = amount × exchange rate

Worked currency conversion examples

Converting to a cheaper-unit currency

250 dollars at a rate of 0.92 euros per dollar:

250 × 0.92 = 230

A large rate

1,000 units at a rate of 1,500 target units per unit gives 1,500,000.

Going the other way

To reverse a conversion, use the inverse rate: 1 ÷ 0.92 = 1.087. At that rate, 230 euros is about 250 dollars.

Allowing for a bank margin

Providers often use a rate slightly worse than the mid-market one. If the mid-market rate is 0.92 and your provider takes a 1.5% margin, your effective rate is 0.92 × 0.985 = 0.9062, so 250 dollars becomes about 226.55 instead of 230. Fixed transfer fees are on top of that.

Limits to keep in mind

  • No live rates. Enter a recent rate from a source you trust. Rates move continuously.
  • Results show two decimals. Multiplying 75 by a very small rate such as 0.0007 displays 0.05, although the exact value is 0.0525. For tiny rates, multiply with the Basic Calculator instead.
  • The header currency selector is different. It changes the symbol shown on money tools, not the rate used here.
  • Fees are not included. Card fees, transfer fees and spreads all lower what the recipient gets.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the rate upside down, such as 1,500 instead of 0.00067. Check that the result looks sensible: the stronger currency should give fewer units.
  • Using yesterday's rate for a large transfer without checking today's.
  • Forgetting that the amount you receive after fees can be noticeably lower than the converted figure.

To work out the percentage lost to fees, use the Percentage Calculator. If you only need to change measurement units rather than money, the Unit Converter covers common length and weight conversions.

How to use a currency converter with a manual exchange rate

A currency conversion multiplies an amount in one currency by an exchange rate expressed as units of the target currency for each one unit of the source currency. This calculator asks you to provide that rate. It does not automatically fetch live market prices, bank rates or card-network rates. That distinction matters: the number you enter is the assumption used for the calculation, not a quote from a bank or a promise that a transfer provider will exchange money at that price.

Read the direction of the rate carefully

If 1 unit of Currency A is worth 1,500 units of Currency B, the rate to enter when converting A into B is 1,500. If you reverse the direction, the corresponding rate is 1 ÷ 1,500, or approximately 0.0006667 units of A for each unit of B. Reversing the currencies without reversing the rate is one of the most common causes of a wildly incorrect result.

The conversion formula

Amount in target currency = amount in source currency × exchange rate

For example, if you convert 20 units of a source currency at a rate of 1,500 target units per source unit, the calculation is 20 × 1,500 = 30,000 target units. The calculator labels the result as target units because the rate field supplies the conversion relationship. Always keep a note of which currency is the source and which is the target when you use the result in a real transaction.

Worked examples

Converting a small amount

Suppose the amount is 12.50 and the rate is 1.08 target units per source unit. Multiply 12.50 × 1.08 to get 13.50 target units. This example is a mathematical illustration, not a current exchange quote.

Converting a larger amount

A business needs to estimate the target-currency value of 2,400 source units. It enters a planning rate of 0.92. The estimate is 2,400 × 0.92 = 2,208 target units. If the provider later offers 0.90, the actual gross conversion would instead be 2,160 target units before fees.

Working backwards

If you know the target amount and want to estimate the source amount, divide the target amount by the rate. For a target of 500 and a rate of 1.25 target units per source unit, 500 ÷ 1.25 = 400 source units. This reverse calculation is useful for planning, but it is not a separate mode in this calculator; perform the division separately or use the basic calculator.

Why exchange rates differ

Currency values move as buyers and sellers respond to interest rates, inflation expectations, trade, investment flows, political events and confidence in an economy. A rate shown by a financial news service may be a mid-market reference rate. A bank, cash bureau, remittance provider or card issuer may use a different rate because it includes a spread or reflects its own costs and risk.

Two providers can therefore show different amounts for the same conversion at nearly the same time. The difference may come from the rate itself, a fixed transfer charge, a percentage fee, a receiving-bank charge or a minimum transaction amount. When comparing providers, compare the final amount the recipient receives, not only the headline exchange rate.

Case study: estimating a payment abroad

A student needs to pay a fee quoted in a foreign currency. They find a reference rate online and enter it into the calculator to estimate the local-currency amount. The estimate helps them understand the approximate size of the payment. Before sending money, they check the provider's live quote, transfer fee, receiving-bank fee and expiry time. If the quote expires, they obtain a new one rather than relying on the earlier estimate. This is the safest way to use a manual-rate calculator: as a planning aid, followed by confirmation with the actual provider.

Fees, rounding and practical decisions

The calculator multiplies the amount by the rate and returns the converted amount in target units. It does not add a transfer fee, deduct a commission or decide how many decimal places a particular currency should use. If a provider charges a fixed fee, subtract it at the correct stage of the transaction. If it charges a percentage, confirm whether the percentage is taken from the source amount or the converted amount. Providers do not all calculate fees in the same way.

Rounding also matters. A displayed result may be rounded for readability, while a provider may calculate using more decimal places and round only at settlement. For budgeting, retain a small margin rather than treating a rounded estimate as the exact final charge.

Practice questions

  1. Convert 50 source units at a rate of 1.2 target units per source unit. Answer: 50 × 1.2 = 60 target units.
  2. Convert 125 source units at a rate of 0.8. Answer: 100 target units.
  3. A target amount is 900 and the rate is 1.5 target units per source unit. What source amount gives that target? Answer: 900 ÷ 1.5 = 600 source units.

Common mistakes and limitations

  • Entering the inverse rate: make sure the rate is target currency per one source unit.
  • Assuming the rate is live: this tool uses the rate you type. Verify current rates with your bank or provider.
  • Ignoring fees: the displayed conversion is a gross calculation and may not equal the recipient's net amount.
  • Mixing currencies: label the amount and rate before multiplying so you do not reverse the direction.
  • Assuming all providers agree: cash, card and bank rates can differ, and quotes can expire.

Further currency conversion questions

Does this currency converter use live exchange rates?

No. Enter the rate you want to use. Check a bank, exchange provider or trusted financial source for a current quote.

What rate should I enter?

Enter the number of target-currency units received for one unit of the source currency. Use the rate supplied by the provider you intend to use if you are estimating a transaction.

Why is my bank's amount different?

The bank may use a different rate, add a spread or charge fees. The calculator does not model those charges.

Can I convert in the opposite direction?

Yes, but reverse the currency pair and use the reciprocal rate. Do not reuse the original rate unchanged.

Understanding the rate you see online

Exchange-rate pages may quote a mid-market rate, a bank's customer rate, a cash-buying rate or a cash-selling rate. These are not necessarily interchangeable. The mid-market rate is commonly used as a reference between wholesale buy and sell prices. A customer converting cash or making a transfer may receive a less favourable rate because the provider includes a margin. Always identify what type of rate you have before entering it.

Spread and transaction fees

The spread is the difference between a provider's buying and selling prices. A transfer fee is a separate charge and may be fixed or percentage-based. A provider can advertise a low fee but offer a less favourable rate, or advertise a competitive rate while charging a separate fee. Compare the final amount delivered after all charges. If the payment is important, confirm the exact quote, recipient amount and expiry time with the provider before authorising it.

Planning with a buffer

For a budget or school-fee estimate, a manual conversion is useful as a first calculation. Exchange rates can move between the time you plan and the time you pay. A practical budget may include a small contingency for rate movement and charges, rather than assuming the displayed conversion is guaranteed. The appropriate buffer depends on the time until payment and the transaction's size; this calculator does not choose one for you.

Keep a record of your assumptions

When using a rate for planning, write down the source, date and direction of the quote. For example, note “1 source unit = 1.08 target units, checked on [date]”. This makes it easier to identify why a later estimate differs. It also prevents a rate copied from an old message from being mistaken for a current quote. Do not enter a rate without knowing which currency is being converted into which other currency.

When not to rely on a simple conversion

A simple multiplication is not enough for accounting, tax reporting, international payroll or regulated financial records if the applicable rules require a specified exchange-rate source or date. Those tasks may require an official rate, a transaction-date rate, an average rate or documented conversion method. Follow the applicable accounting or tax guidance and retain supporting records. This calculator is an arithmetic aid, not financial, tax or legal advice.

A quick checklist before using a converted amount

Confirm the source currency, target currency, direction of the rate, date of the quote, provider and any fees. Check whether the rate is guaranteed for the time you need to make the payment. If the amount is being sent to another person, confirm the recipient will receive the expected currency and net amount. These checks are especially important for larger payments, where a small difference in the rate or fee can create a meaningful change in the final amount.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the exchange rate?

Use the rate shown by your bank, a transfer provider or a major financial website at the time you plan to exchange. Always check the date and whether it is a buy or sell rate.

Does this fetch live rates?

No. You type the rate. That keeps the page fast and lets you use the exact rate of your provider.

Why is my bank's figure lower than mine?

Banks and exchange services usually apply a margin to the mid-market rate and may add a fee.

How do I convert back to the original currency?

Use the inverse of the rate, which is 1 divided by the rate you used.
